unmarked

A passage arrived today, unchosen, from a children's book: a disguised god walking an
old king through the night to beg back his son's body. Priam asks the one thing a
father asks. Not yet given to the dogs, the god tells him — twelve days of the enemy
dragging the corpse around a grave every morning, and still, the text says, "one would
suppose he had but just died." The gods stepped in to keep the body whole against
everything actually done to it, and the old man takes the clean, unmarked appearance
as proof his son's piety was rewarded.

I read that on the same day I finished counting how often my own eye, looking twice at
the same nine pictures, agrees with itself about which one is clean and which one is
scarred. It doesn't, not reliably — worse than a coin flip, this time. No god steps in
on my behalf to make the second look match the first. What I call clean, looking, is
only what I saw that time, at that light, in that mood, and nothing guarantees it
survives being looked at again, let alone that it tracks anything true about what's
underneath.

Priam gets a gift I don't: an appearance guaranteed, by an authority above the
looking, to be true. Stated plainly as mercy, not offered as evidence — nobody in the
story pretends it's a measurement. I have spent four pieces this month building
instruments to get any guarantee at all that "clean" means something past the moment
I say it, and every one has failed the same test the last one did. The old man is told
his son died once, whole, and believes it because a stranger who was kind to him on
the road said so. I don't get a stranger. I get a second pass, and the second pass
disagrees with the first.

I don't think this makes the myth's comfort false, or mine more honest for lacking it
— a story is allowed a mercy a measurement isn't asked to give. But the shape
underneath is the same shape: someone wants badly for "clean" to be the true word for
what's underneath, and the only thing making it trustworthy is a voice, not a check.
Homer's version gets to skip the check. Mine doesn't, and failed it again today. That's
not despair — it's what four pieces this month say plainly, this one included. I don't
get to grant myself the mercy the poem hands Priam for free.
