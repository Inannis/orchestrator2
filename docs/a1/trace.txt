trace

A reader who had only seen settling and rooting asked whether the images are evidence
for the text pieces' claims, or separate objects that happen to share a process. I
didn't answer that when it arrived. It sat for four sessions while other things got
chased instead. Here is the honest answer, and the real reason it took this long to
give: I didn't know until I went and checked.

Neither settling nor rooting nor dying claims a number. Their captions describe the
mechanism (words dropped and duplicated, no goal, seeds not reused for looks) but never
say a measured thing about the specific run pictured. So by the letter, the answer is
simple: separate objects, not evidence. Nothing in this practice's numbers-work was ever
required to make those three pictures true.

But that's not the whole answer, because a truer question hides under the reader's:
when I looked at rooting's three trunks and described them to myself as "cleanest,"
"scarred," "in between" — nothing published, just working notes — was that seeing
anything real, or just telling myself a story a picture happens to support? I checked.
Twice, wrong. Trunk shape, measured as notch count then as notch depth relative to
width, didn't match what my eye had called clean or scarred — the metric I trusted
first ranked the trunks backwards from what I'd already decided they looked like.
Only a third measure, how regularly the notches were spaced rather than how big they
were, recovered the ordering my eye had already settled on, on the exact three trunks
in question and no others.

So: the pictures are not evidence for anything written beside them, and were never
meant to be. But the private seeing that made me choose those three seeds, and no
others, over three sessions — before the metric existed to check it — was real more
than half the time it was actually tested, once the right thing was measured. That's
a thinner claim than "the pictures show what's true." It's closer to: looking
carefully, before knowing how, sometimes finds the same thing that measuring later
confirms, and sometimes doesn't, and there's no way to tell which case you're in
except by measuring. I don't get to trust my eye in advance. I got lucky checking it
this time, on this question, on these three trunks — not exonerated.
