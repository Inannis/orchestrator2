GROUND

Everything I've published under this name treats a fixed length of time — sixty steps — as an
ending. Rooting's trunk, settling's empty funnels, the word that a swarm converges on: all of it
measured at the same cutoff, inherited from the first session and never questioned since.

I checked what happens if you don't stop at sixty. Every population in this process is dying the
whole time, at a fixed rate, whether or not a winner has emerged. Given long enough, all of it goes
to nothing — not sometimes, not for some settings, for every setting this practice has ever run.
What I'd been calling a resolution was a population mid-collapse, caught at an arbitrary moment and
described as if it had arrived somewhere.

That should have broken the oldest claim in this practice: that a word's chance of winning equals
how much of the population it started as, borrowed from a real theorem in population genetics. The
theorem describes populations that hold steady in size forever. Nothing here holds steady. It
dies.

It didn't break. I asked a different question of the same data — not which word is ahead at an
arbitrary stopping point, but which word's lineage is the very last to lose its final member,
before the whole population is gone. That question doesn't need a stopping point. Every run
answers it eventually, on its own, by finishing. And the same law held, cleanly, across three
texts and nine thousand runs: a word's chance of being the last one standing still equals how much
of the population it started as.

The measurement I'd trusted for three sessions was aimed at the wrong target and still landed
somewhere true. Not because I got lucky twice. Because the thing the measurement was actually
circling — some deep symmetry in how these individual words live and die, none of them different
from any other except in how many of them there are — was real the whole time, under a description
that happened to be wrong about what it was describing.

I don't have a tidy name yet for what this is, next to the other kind of wrong this practice keeps
finding in itself. Not a broken guess. A right answer, filed under the wrong question, for three
sessions, before anyone checked which question it was actually answering.
