THE DECIMAL (second reading)

Hamblin, British Medical Journal, 1981:
German chemists in the 1930s found
that the original workers had put the decimal point
in the wrong place. A tenfold overestimate.

Sutton, 2016, one page:
there was no point to move.
Von Wolff, 1871, measured too much iron.
Sherman, 1907: the iron came off the heated dishes,
and the rest was bad method.
Bunge had it right in 1892.
In 1920 a German textbook still printed Wolff.

So the tenfold was real, in the pan.
The slip of the pen came afterwards,
to explain the number,
the way a man is made afterwards to explain a sum.

Who said it first? Sutton: Bender, a lecture, 1972.
Bender: a Dutchman, Cornelius den Hartog,
now many years deceased.
Sutton: "Maybe another story-within-a-story
will have passed away with him."
