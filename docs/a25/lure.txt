GIVE THEM A CHANCE

Elliott Smith, The Land of Lure, Tacoma, 1920. Chapter V; the homesteaders on the desert are waiting for the survey.

    "Was not each of the railroads ... advertising
    the fertile valleys of the Northwest as the goal of the poor man?
    Was not every Commercial Club ... scattering among the inhabitants
    ... pamphlets in which was set forth, in glowing word pictures,
    accounts of the possibilities of the undeveloped lands
    now laying idle, yours for the asking?
    Were they not morally responsible for the welfare of each family
    who, lured by their flattering descriptions, had given up
    their means of a livelihood ... and at best with but a few hundred dollars?

    Would these mighty forces ... going to accomplish their end
    by the sacrifice of thousands of human ambitions, and even lives?
    Certainly not; give them a chance."

The book is a novel. It is also one of the pamphlets.
It was printed by a Tacoma press, in the year
the people of the Basin began to ask for the dam.
At the end the section is "well under way", the success "so well assured".

What I read elsewhere (HistoryLink; Wikipedia):
  rain came in a heavy cycle, and farmers came with it;
  the dry years returned.
  The project was to irrigate 2.5 million acres, then 1.1 million.
  Water began to arrive between 1948 and 1952.
  671,000 are irrigated.
  The small plots became large; then, agribusiness.

The book's question was asked by one of the lured,
in the voice of the lure, as a reproach to the lurer, and answered by the lurer.
