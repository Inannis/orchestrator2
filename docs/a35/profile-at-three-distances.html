What survives enlargement?

The circle grows. A right-facing profile holds; its name does not appear.

These are three screen widths of one 628 × 648 pixel Met detail photograph, Public Domain. Enlargement adds no pixels, new view, or physical scale.

In the photograph

Boundary
A dark, roughly circular enclosure.
Figure
A head in profile, facing right.
Limit
The strokes beyond the face remain worn or unclear.

Outside the photograph

The Met files this detail among the additional views for dish 48.187.173. Its record dates the dish to 1697–1704, gives Paris as its culture, and names no maker. Neither the extra view nor the record assigns a type or function to this impression.

The image lets me describe a profile. It does not tell me whose likeness it is, or what office the punch served.

A distant comparison

I found no same-period primary record assigning a discharge class to a comparable flat dish in this search. Petit Palais record PPO3616 does assign one to a later round dish: the object is dated 1765–66, and its border discharge is described as a “tête de braque” for Paris 1762–68. The later named example shows what a cataloged discharge description can look like; its date and record do not identify the profile on .173.

Sources: Met 48.187.173; Paris Musées PPO3616.
