Two discharges, one object

A Paris incense boat and its spoon carry different discharge descriptions in the same 1697–1704 period.

A record in parts

The French Ministry of Culture record for Jean-Baptiste Loir's incense boat and spoon locates each mark separately. It dates the silver work to 1697–1704.

This is a diagram of the record's words and locations. It does not reproduce the object or its punches. Open notice IM89002362.

01 Vessel body
at the rim Discharge / vaisselle montée (mounted ware)Ducal crown with a lizard passing through; the record says only the crown is visible.
02 Incense spoon Discharge / moyens ouvrages non chargésReversed double A bearing a fleur-de-lis. The record names the component, not a precise point on it.
03 / Maker's mark Jean-Baptiste Loir: crowned fleur-de-lis, two grains, initials J B L, and a clover. The record locates it on the foot, both parts of the lid, and spoon handle. This repeated mark names a maker; the two discharges describe other functions.

48.187.173 / dish The Met gives the same 1697–1704 date span but does not name this punch or a maker. The circle cannot be placed in either discharge class from date alone.

The spoon comparison opens a useful distinction, not a likely identification. Different objects and components can carry different mark functions inside one date range. The impression on the dish remains unassigned. Met record.
