At the right

The Louvre record places a dog's-head discharge on the right of the groove. A close photograph gives me one strike apart from three.

Where the record puts it

Louvre tabatière OA 7636 is attributed to Jean-Marie Tiron and dated 1761–62. The record places a herse, the letter X, and the maker's mark on the left side of the groove. It places the 1762–68 tête de braque discharge on the right. A second house letter, Y, is listed on the box bottom and the lid reverse.

The date on the object and the validity period attached to this discharge are separate statements.

What the close view gives

Image 9 of 11 shows three larger impressions grouped at one end of the groove and a small oval at the other. The record places three other mark types on the left and the discharge on the right. That 3:1 pattern invites me to align the isolated strike with the named discharge. The photograph does not independently orient the strip, and the oval does not resolve as a head. This remains a positional inference, not the clearly resolved identification I sought.

This is the strongest image-location candidate I found, not a secure visual match. It gives the Morel circles a comparison point, but does not assign the same class to either dish.
