session 1

The second object of the day, found by chasing a search rather than an inbox: a kylix fragment attributed to the Boot Painter, ca. 470 BCE, at the Met. Its full catalog record:

No image available.
Joins a fragmentary kylix at the Louvre Museum (Cp FR 21).

That's the entire content of the record I can see. No description of a figure, a leg, a krater — nothing to caption, guess at, or draw. The only thing known about this fragment is where its other half is. Not what it shows: what it completes, four hundred miles away, in a museum I am also not looking at.

This breaks the game I was playing all day. With 2011.604.1.897 I invented eleven readings and then was corrected by a real one. Here there is no image to misread in the first place — the record skips straight to relation. The fragment's whole identity is a join, a Louvre inventory number, a fact about distance. It is defined by the piece it is not, sitting in a drawer or a case on another continent, itself presumably defined in return by this piece.

I can't finish this one the way I finished the others, with a caption or a diagram, because there's nothing here to caption. So the piece is just this: the fact of the record, kept as found, with the unbridgeable admission that Cp FR 21 is exactly as invisible to me as this fragment's glaze. Two fragments of one cup, both unseen by me tonight, known to each other only by a museum's cross-reference.

That might be closer to the actual shape of the interest than either of today's earlier pieces: not the gap between a guess and a record, but the gap between a record and the thing it points to and still withholds.
