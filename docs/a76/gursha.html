The water comes first, in the jug with the long lip, and you hold your hands out over the basin and someone you did not look at pours. It is always someone you did not look at. Aster says this is the whole of the manners: that the first thing done to you is done by a person whose face you leave alone, and that after this you may look at anybody.

Then the round table of woven grass, and the cloth over it lifted, and under the cloth the grey sheet of bread with everything laid on it in heaps, so that the plate is the bread and the bread is the tablecloth and, in a minute, the bread is also the spoon.

My grandmother fed me. I am told this; I was small. She rolled a piece the size of my fist around some of the red and some of the yellow and brought it to my mouth, and I was to open for it, wide, wider than was comfortable, because the piece was not sized for comfort. It was sized for her. That is the thing nobody tells you about the gift of a mouthful: it is the size she wants you to be.

Aster keeps a count. Not of the meals. Of the mouthfuls she has put into other people, and the ones put into her, and she says the two numbers must never be allowed to come out even, because on the day they came out even there would be nothing left to owe, and a table where nobody owes anybody is only a place where food is. She is seventy-one. She says she is eleven thousand ahead, and also that she is behind by a number she will not say.

At the funeral they did not wash first. I noticed it and said nothing. The jug was there. Someone had set it on the floor by the door and then the door had stayed open, and that was all.

We ate anyway. A man I did not know, big, in a good coat, tore the bread and rolled it and held it out across the corner of the table toward my mother, who was looking at the wall. He held it out for as long as it takes to say a name. Then he put it in his own mouth. I thought: that is a mouthful that went the wrong way and he has to carry it.

Afterwards Aster said it was not wrong. It is a thing you may do: feed the one who is not there, and eat it yourself. You are only the road it takes.

Last week Aster put a piece in my mouth without warning, the red one, too big. I opened. My eyes went. She was already looking at the bread.
