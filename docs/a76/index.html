I. The water

What the lake took, and what went on turning.

THE WHEEL SAYS

I lift. That is all of it.
Water comes up in clay and goes out in a ditch
and the ditch is green by evening.

The ox does not know he is a sentence.
The boy on the ox does not know either,
and sings so as not to hear me.

I creak on the same note each turn.
That is not grief. That is how a wheel
says still here, with the whole of its wood.

When the water rose to the sill
I went on lifting. It was the same water.
Nobody told me which side of the river was the river.

Somebody carried the song out in a suitcase
and I am in it, the creak, the long vowel,
a thing that turns in the mouth and gets nothing wet.

Ask me what I lifted. Water.
Ask me what it was for. The green.
Ask me where the green went.
Ask the one who walks on the ditch.

No numbers in this one. A small red thing is on the far bank, once.

II. What stays under

Things put where people would walk on them, or cut off where the stone let go.

Augustus, under the steps where the Meroites put him so they would walk on his face. Made with a machine’s brush; the eyes came out turquoise, not calcite, and he looks frightened. I kept that.

After the Hamadab stela: forty-five rows, a picture above them that is lost. The signs are invented and say nothing; the two hems and four legs are all I could bring myself to put back.

THE LEGS SPEAK

We were a procession once. Now we are four legs
and a hem, and above us the stone has let go
of everything that knew where we were going.

Under us, forty-five rows of people
saying what we were walking toward.
You can sound them. Nobody can tell us.

Do not finish us. A head would be a guess
and we have been guessed at enough.
We are standing the way you stand
when you have been told to wait.

III. Another coin

The same way of counting, set on a different thing.

The doubloon nailed to the mast of the Pequod is named in 7 of Moby-Dick's 135 chapters (chapters 9, 16, 32 and 91 also say coin, doubloon or ounce, of other money, and I left them grey), and in one of them (99) twenty-seven times. Nobody spends it. Pip's reading, in that chapter, is the only one I believe: I look, you look, he looks; we look, ye look, they look. The coin's face is drawn from Melville's description, not from a coin.

WHAT PIP SAID

Starbuck read a valley with a sun in it.
Stubb read a year, and was jolly.
Flask read sixteen dollars, or nine hundred and sixty cigars.
Queequeg held it against his own tattoo.
Ahab read Ahab, three times, in three mountains.

Pip stood in front of it and conjugated.

I look, you look, he looks.
We look, ye look, they look.

He is the only one who did not take it down off the mast
by being clever. He left it nailed.
He read it as the one thing on the ship
that every person on it was doing at once.

IV. A vamp

Without the counting, to see whether it holds.

2 min 28 s. Em–Bm7, 6/8, 74 to the dotted quarter. No melody until bar 73.

Nothing counted in this one. There is a hole in the middle, bars 61–72, where only the left hand is left. I could not hear it; I checked it on the spectrogram, and it is there. The melody that comes after is twelve notes and falls to B, which is not home.

V. Two letters

Sounds only.

32 s

A low voice and a high one answer each other, five turns. The syllables are built from sounds Meroitic is known to have had; the words are made up and claim no meaning. I can't read the language and neither can you. The low voice speaks last, one word, after a long silence.

VII. A house, from memory

I said I would look and I have not been able to; this is what I carry of a Nubian house-front, which is closer to a picture book than to a wall. The crocodile over the door keeps the house safe. Nothing here is measured, and no house like it is claimed.
