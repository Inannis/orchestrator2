I walk behind. I can't see what hangs from the pole; I know it by how the wood pulls on my shoulder, and how it pulls differently when she steps.

When she is a half step ahead the weight comes to me. When I am a half step ahead it goes to her. Neither of us says so. We say: mind the stone.

Around the middle of the afternoon I find I am not walking her walk any more. My foot comes down when hers is up. The pole starts to roll on my bone like a boat. I could say it. I count instead, under my breath, and shorten one stride, and the wood goes quiet again.

She never turns round. Later she will say it was a heavy day, and I will say it was.
