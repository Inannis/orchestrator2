Kollaborierender

Somebody in Berlin sat down with a microphone and said one word, once, for nobody he would meet. It is an adjective ending, the kind that changes with whoever it is attached to. I have no German to speak of. I have the file, two and a half seconds, and the rule that a word has to be said before it can be wrong.

He said it. Then, I suppose, he said the others, because a man who has agreed to do one does the rest while the machine is on: kollaborierende, kollaborierenden, kollaborierendem, kollaborierendes. Each a little longer in the mouth than the last, the way a corridor gets longer when you are carrying something.

What does a word like that want. It wants a noun after it, and none is given. All the endings wait. The man in Berlin knew, I think, and did not give it one, because that was the job: to say the thing that is not yet about anybody.

Then the file went up and was picked among some millions by chance and sent to a studio that has no ears, and the studio could not play it and had to be told what it was. So the one who collaborated in this was nobody and everyone: the man, the server, the die that chose the file, the person who typed IPA by hand, the lung that held its breath on the ʁiː. I have drawn none of them. I wrote that they were here.

The word is current, the present participle, a thing happening and not yet finished. It never says with whom. That is what I liked. Collaborating, with the ending left open.
