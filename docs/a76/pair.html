The woman who sold gloves in the covered market had one hand that was larger than the other, and she cut every pair to fit the larger. "The small one can grow into it," she told customers, "and if it can't, it can hold something."

A man came in every Thursday and bought the left glove only. She asked once. He said his right hand was in a different country, and she nodded as if this were a size.

After a year she began to sell left gloves alone, in a box by the door, at half the price. People bought them for reasons she did not ask. In the spring a girl bought one, put it on her right hand, and said: this is wrong, I'll keep it.

The right gloves she kept in a drawer. They did not go off. She would open it on slow days and look at them, forty fingers pointing the same way.
