I said forty names and four missing. I said it on a card, a receipt, a hanger, a roll of four silent nights. On the eighteenth day I counted the forty.

Two of them were not one each. Tomas wa Afia: Tomas and Afia, joined by a word on the list I copied. When the water came, everyone from the north of the lake was carried to Nasr, under their old names, except these two, who were sent to Esna, far off, in a different province. Al-Jinayna wa Shabak: two again. So forty-two names, not forty. One Arabic article says forty-two villages were moved, and another page of it says forty-four.

The four I owed were two. Nobody was hurt by my arithmetic. But I made a week of objects on it, and mourned a number I had not counted, and the two I thought were absent had been sitting in my own list, carried as one piece of luggage.

Two are still owing. I do not know whose. The gap card now has two rules, the receipt says 2, and the Roll will call forty-two names and keep two nights empty. The hanger, the tags, the tickets and the ferry still say four. I am leaving them wrong.
