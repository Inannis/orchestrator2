The Pit

One bell, cast in one winter in Bohemia, 1420; the place invented. Pictures lit only by the metal or the torch; the image model drew, I took the light and the dark. Plates are shown as they are made, not in order.

Century 1. The pit is dug first, and the bell is not yet. Men measure the hollow by a body that has no body; the earth they lift is the first thing the bell is made of, which is its absence. I had thought want a poverty. It is the very measure the gift is fitted to: no one digs a pit for what he can do without.

Century 2. The board does not make the bell; it only refuses to let the clay be anywhere but where the bell is not. Turn it once and the curve is there, a shape cut out of a shape. So with every want I have had: I did not build it, I wiped away what was not it, and what was left stood up and was called by a name.

Century 3. The false bell is made to be destroyed, and it is lettered all the same, because the letters will be written into the true one backward, by fire, in a place no hand can reach. The words on it are the model's and are not a language; I leave them. A man says what he means to the clay and does not live to read the answer in metal.

Century 4. The core is dried for days over a fire too low to be called one, and the man who keeps it does not look at the clay. He holds out his hand and takes the heat on the back of it, as a mother tests milk. What goes out of the mould is water; what he is waiting for is the moment it has all gone. I have wanted things with a wet heart and been surprised, after, by how they cracked. Patience here is only this: to be warm enough, for long enough, and no warmer.

Century 5. She does not throw it in. She holds it out first, on the flat of the hand, so that someone may see how little it is, and then she holds it a moment longer. A spoon has been in a mouth every day of a life; it has the shape of the hollow it fed. They will not find it again in the bell, and no one will ring it and say that is Anna's spoon. Yet the note will be a little different for it, by a thickness no one can measure. What is given in the dark is not lost: it is only no longer separate.

The lantern is the model's, and too modern; I left it. Her name is invented.

Century 6. Before the metal moves it is read by colour alone: red is too early, white is too late, and between them is a yellow that has no name in any language I know. The founder cannot weigh it or taste it. He looks, and the whole labour of the winter is staked on a thing he sees with an eye that is tired. I have learned to be afraid of a love that is certain and to trust the one that is merely looking carefully at a colour.

The model drew a man beside the furnace in a modern shirt; I took him out into the dark.

Century 7. Nobody speaks at the pour. Four faces lean over a light that is brighter than any of them and every one of them is lit by it on the side toward the hole, which is the only side that has ever been true. They have given their spoons and their winter; what they look at now is not metal but the place where the metal will have been a bell. I thought a want was a dark. Here it is the one bright thing in the room, and all the faces are turned to it as to a child.

Plate 7, the pour: What the Pit Wants. Made first.

Century 8. The metal has gone in and there is nothing to do. The mould is packed with earth, the earth is covered, and a man is set to stand by it, because someone must be near a thing that is cooling. He cannot see it. He can see the snow gone black in a ring, and the steam, and now and then his own breath, which is the other warm thing here. A bell that is uncovered too soon will crack. Four days is what the founder in my head says; I have not found it in any book, and the watchman does not count.

Plate 8. The model drew the man in a modern coat; I put him in the dark and kept his breath. The only light is the heat of the mound.

Century 9. The mould is the only part of the work that is made to be broken, and it is broken with a hammer, from the top, by someone who has not slept. Under the clay the bell is not bronze-coloured. It is the green-black of a thing that has been inside, and it has not yet made one sound. It is, for an hour, the quietest bell there will ever be. Then the daylight reaches it, and someone has to decide who strikes it first.

Plate 9. The letters are the model's, not a language. The daylight is a relit shaft; the pit around it is crushed to dark.
