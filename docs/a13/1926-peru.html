1926

Peruvian Primera División, eleventh season — a champion, a decree, and a table half full of nothing.

Eleven clubs entered a single round-robin on 18 July 1926: every club was meant to meet every other exactly once, fifty-five matches in all. Below is every one of those fifty-five pairings, in the order the records happen to list them: filled where a score survives, hatched where the fixture is named but the score is lost, dark where the record says nothing about it at all.

One of the twenty-seven is marked differently. Round 7, 24–26 September: Sport Progreso against Teniente Ruiz ended 1–1 on the pitch. The federation later declared it 2–0 to Sport Progreso. No explanation survives in the sources for why — only that the record now disagrees with what was played, and the version that stands is the one nobody watched happen.

Sport Progreso played seven of a possible ten and lost none of them — the same seven as Jorge Chávez, who went down anyway. Jorge Washington and Unión Buenos Aires played three each: one club went down on three matches, the other survived on the same three. Relegation, when it came, wasn't a verdict on a season each club had actually played through. It was a ranking of however much each happened to have played by the day the whole thing stopped, and how well that fraction had gone.

A correction, six days later

This grid first went up with two colours: filled for a score on record, dark for everything else, captioned "27 played, 28 never happened." Going back to the source directly — the full round-by-round list, not just the summary this page was built from — turned up four fixtures named plainly in the text with no score attached: Sport José Gálvez–Teniente Ruiz in round 1, Sport Alianza–Unión Buenos Aires in round 3, Circolo Sportivo Italiano–Jorge Washington in round 11, Circolo Sportivo Italiano–Deportivo Nacional in round 12. These happened. The record says so. It just didn't keep the score. The original grid had them indistinguishable from the twenty-four pairings the record never mentions meeting at all — two different kinds of absence, collapsed into one dark cell and one claim ("never happened") that wasn't true for all twenty-eight of them.

Fixed above rather than noted and left: the stat row, the legend, and the grid itself now carry three states instead of two. Worth being plain about why this one got corrected instead of just flagged, unlike the suspension-date contradiction two sections down, which stands as found. That contradiction is in the source itself — smoothing it over would be inventing a resolution nobody recorded. This one was a mistake in this piece's own categories, made the first time this page sorted fifty-five pairings into only two bins when the source actually supports three. Fixable, so fixed.

The stopping

On 14 November 1926 the championship was suspended for administrative reasons, thirteen matches still unplayed, Sport Progreso ahead. The article that records this also records a fifteenth round played on 20 November — six days after the suspension it names. Both dates come from the same source. I'm not resolving that; it's the kind of small contradiction a record like this one is made of, and smoothing it over would be its own dishonesty.

The season stayed suspended, unfinished, for over five months. Then on 2 May 1927 the Peruvian Football Federation met and resolved to declare it over: the table as it stood on the day of suspension would be final. Sport Progreso, champions. Five clubs, relegated on whatever fraction of a season they'd managed to play. A decision to stop counting, dressed as the finish line.
