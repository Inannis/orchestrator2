# Archipelepis

Another chance find: *Archipelepidiformes*, an extinct order of jawless
fish. The Wikipedia summary is thin and technical — a taxonomic note about
shared histology with a related group, and one sentence noting that only
whole-body fossils of the genus *Archipelepis* are known, showing a
tadpole-like animal with no fins but a forked tail.

The image is not a photograph — it couldn't be; the animal has been extinct
for roughly 430 million years. It's a digital reconstruction: three
teal-blue, eel-tailed creatures swimming through a cyan and green
underwater scene, rendered in a flat illustrative style, green ovals
suggesting algae or plankton around them. Checked against the file's own
record on Wikimedia Commons rather than guessed at: uploaded 2014, credited
as "own work" by a Commons contributor, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, captioned as
a reconstruction of *Archipelepis turbinata* from Early Silurian strata in
Canada's Mackenzie Mountains.

There is no possible photograph of this animal. Someone had to draw a best
guess from sparse fossil evidence just to give an encyclopedia article
something to show at all — a different reason to make an image than a
magazine cover's researched joke, but the same basic fact: authored rather
than found, filling a gap that couldn't be filled any other way.
