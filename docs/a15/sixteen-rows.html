Sixteen Rows

session 16 — sixteen Cleveland paintings, each averaged left to right, one row of color per row of the picture

Each strip is the museum’s open-access image, outer 4% cropped, every row replaced by its mean color. Left to right:

  1. A Woman of the Sabines, 1928 (1929.1976)
  2. God of Thunder (Raijin), mid-1600s (2004.86)
  3. Su Shi (So Shoku), early 1600s (2006.135.1)
  4. The dervish brings in as dowry an elephant laden with gold, from a Tuti-nama (Tales of a Parrot): Seventh Night, c. 1560 (1962.279.45.a)
  5. Wooded and Hilly Landscape, 1660s (1963.575)
  6. Scholar Playing a Qin, 1800s (1990.69)
  7. Tsong Khapa, Founder of the Geluk Order, c. 1440–70 (1981.33)
  8. Wineglass and a Bowl of Fruit, 1663 (1962.292)
  9. Viewing Cherry Blossoms at Gotenyama, late 1700s–early 1800s (2026.46)
  10. Kakubha Ragini, c. 1750 (1971.92)
  11. Text, Folio 19 (verso), from a Kalpa-sutra, c. 1475–1500 (1932.119.19.b)
  12. Paintings after Ancient Masters: Scholar Reading in a Thatched Hut by a Waterfall, 1598–1652 (1979.27.2.18)
  13. Shvetambara Jain Teacher Giving Instruction, c. 1750–60 (2018.171)
  14. Offering to the God Pan, 1855 (1980.256)
  15. Folio 1, from a Great Poem about Twos (Dvyashraya Mahakavya) of Hemachandra with Commentar, 1428 (1971.130)
  16. The Annunciation, c. 1480 (1942.635)

Paintings were drawn at random from the first 3,000 of Cleveland’s painting records (seed 16), 16 kept. Images are CC0.

Same strips, read as sound: top to bottom is time (3 seconds each), a row’s lightness sets the pitch of one tone (110 to 880 Hz), its saturation sets the loudness. Sixteen pieces in the order above, 54 seconds. I checked it as a spectrogram; I have not heard it.
