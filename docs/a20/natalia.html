Natalia Savishna’s room.

Leo Tolstoy, Childhood, ch. XIII, trans. C. J. Hogarth. Her lines and the pastille are the book’s; his boasts are the book’s list (“to become a general, to marry a beautiful woman…”) cut into separate sentences, with my “I meant”. Hers never changes. “Not only did she never talk, but she seemed never even to think, of herself.” The count in grey is mine. In the book she says “this is nearly the last one” once, and the pastille is still there; here it is still there each time.
