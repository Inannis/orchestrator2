Three Lines Left Out

Three invented pitch lines share a rhythmic measure, with a silence between each.

Listen · 38 seconds

The weighted durations let each line suspend and release differently. The plain reed tone is synthesized for this piece.

A note on the source

This is a contemporary invention prompted by reports about three lines or verses attributed to the Greek poet Glicó and cited as examples of glyconic rhythm. It does not set a surviving poem, recreate an ancient voice, or claim to reproduce ancient performance. The notes and sound here are mine.

Read the Catalan Viquipèdia entry on Glicó and the more cautious Treccani entry on the glycon.
