The Daughter Carries the Song

A song for the sister's small labor in The Juniper Tree: gathering what remains, carrying it to the root, and hearing what the bird makes of it.

At winter's door an apple split;
the snow kept every red mark.
I folded silk around the bones
and carried them out through dark.

I laid the silk at the root.
The juniper held the ground.
Its branches clapped once in the mist;
a bird made a voice from the sound.

Gold went first to the goldsmith,
red shoes went under my chair;
the mill men lifted the stone
and sent the song through the air.

Now two cups stand on the table.
His hand is warm in mine.
The tree keeps the snow and the blossom;
we eat in their crossing time.

After the Brothers Grimm's “The Juniper-Tree,” KHM 47, in the Taylor and Edwardes English edition. Return to the panel.
