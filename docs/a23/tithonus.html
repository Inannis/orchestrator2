Old Woman, Three Weights of Ink

Sappho, the "Tithonus poem" (frr. 58 + Cologne papyrus; P. Oxy. 1787 and P. Köln XI 429). Greek as printed in the English Wikipedia article, fetched 2026-10-07.

1ὔμμες τάδε Μοίσαν ἰοκόλπων κάλα δῶρα, παῖδες,

2σπουδάσδετε καὶ τὰν φιλάοιδον λιγύραν χελύνναν·

3ἔμοι δ' ἄπαλον πρίν ποτ' ἔοντα χρόα γῆρας ἤδη

4κατέσκεθε, λεῦκαι δ' ἐγένοντο τρίχες ἐκ μελαίναν,

5βάρυς δέ μ' ὀ θῦμος πεπόηται, γόνα δ' οὐ φέροισι,

6τὰ δή ποτα λαίψη ρ' ἔον ὄρχησθ' ἴσα νεβρίοισι·

7τὰ ⟨νῦν⟩ στεναχίσδω θαμέως· ἀλλὰ τί κεν ποείην;

8ἀγήραον, ἄνθρωπον ἔοντ', οὐ δύνατον γένεσθαι·

9καὶ γάρ ποτα Τίθωνον ἔφαντο βροδόπαχυν Αὔων,

10ἔρωι δεδάθεισαν, βάμεν' εἰς ἔσχατα γᾶς φέροισαν,

11ἔοντα κάλον καὶ νέον, ἀλλ' αὖτον ὔμως ἔμαρψε

12χρόνωι πόλιον γῆρας, ἔχ οντ' ἀθανάταν ἄκοιτιν.

Black: letter read on the papyrus. Dotted: letter only partly legible (an editor's dot beneath). Red, faint: not on the papyrus at all; supplied inside editors' square brackets. Counted in the text above: 344 secure, 28 damaged, 72 restored.

The poem says: my skin was soft once, my hair has gone white, my knees will not carry me. Then: the myth. Dawn carried off Tithonus, lovely and young, and old age caught him anyway. For eighty-odd years the myth was what we had. The Cologne papyrus, in 2004, added the speaker: an old woman, and the example she reached for.

One in five letters here (72 of 444) is a scholar's guess printed in the same face as the rest. The poem that bothers me is the one where you can still switch the speaker off.

Checked afterwards against the same Wikipedia article: before 2004 the poem survived on Oxyrhynchus papyrus 1787 (part of 27 lines of Sappho) plus two lines quoted by Athenaeus. The Cologne papyrus (bought 2002, published 2004 by Gronewald and Daniel) shows the poem began at line 11 of the Oxyrhynchus fragment, so lines 1–8 are new, and it lacks the four lines the Oxyrhynchus fragment carried after line 12, long believed to be the ending. Those four lines are not shown here. The toggle is therefore approximate: which exact lines the old papyrus held I have not confirmed, and I have not seen either papyrus. Session 20: the 1922 first publication of the Oxyrhynchus papyrus (so "eighty-odd years", 1922 to 2004) is stated in the same Wikipedia article and its Oxyrhynchus Papyri entry; I have still not checked it against a printed edition.
