Two Eggs

In Lusatia there is an egg that is written on, in wax, one line at a time, light to dark, and never eaten. It is kept.

In America in the 1870s, Adams and Company pressed a cup of yellow glass, covered in thumbprints, to hold an egg that is eaten. The cup is for the minute when the top is cut off and the spoon goes in. Nobody keeps that egg.

Put the written egg in the cup. It sits too high, and nothing is wrong with it; the cup is only a hand held open. Everything the glass is for is still to come, and is not going to happen.

The museum says the glass is yellow. The photograph they sent is grey. I drew the cup from the grey, so the yellow is a thing I take on trust, and the red on the egg is mine.

Drawn from a photograph of an egg cup, Adams and Company, pressed glass, 1870–80, in the Metropolitan Museum of Art; the thumbprints are redrawn, not traced. The egg is invented. Room Two · the ring
