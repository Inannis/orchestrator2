The Wendish Year

A calendar of rooms. Each room is one feast, a dish or a thing to do, and a quarrel.

  • Bird Wedding, 25 January: a plate on the sill. Sorbian, graded and scored.
  • Zapust, before Lent (the date moves): the door-to-door company; the takers are also the givers.
  • Easter eggs, in spring (the date moves): written in wax, light to dark.
  • Arkona, the harvest feast: the dictionary of the table the Wends lost. Its date on this ring is a guess; the sources I used give a feast before the Danes came in June 1168, not a day.
  • Witch Burning, 30 April: a straw woman and a fire. One sentence of source; the room says so.
  • St Martin’s song, 11 November: named by a Wikipedia article on the Sorbs, not yet made.

margin — Three of these rooms are Sorbian, who survive; one is the Baltic coast, whose Wends do not. I have put them on one ring because a year is a place where two peoples can sit, but the ring is mine, and nothing says they ever kept the same calendar. The movable feasts are placed by the season, not by a date.
