A Plate on the SillPtači kwas, the Birds’ Wedding — Room One of the Wendish Year

A custom, a score and a quarrel. Night of 24 January to the morning of the 25th. For one child and one grown person, or for one person who remembers being the child.

AWhat the custom is

On the eve of 25 January the children of Lusatia, the Sorbs, set a plate on the windowsill or in front of the door. In the morning there are sweets on it in the shape of birds and nests: dough magpies under icing (sroki), meringues that are dry outside and soft and sticky within (Schmätzl, or Tschiepchen, “chicks”), cream nests with sugar eggs. The birds have brought them. They are paying: the children fed them in winter, and the birds are giving the wedding breakfast back. In the nursery and the school the children dress as birds and play the wedding itself. In the Sorbian version the bride is the magpie and the groom the raven, not the blackbird and thrush of the German song, and sometimes they wear the Sorbian wedding costume instead of feathers.

Read from the German Wikipedia article Vogelhochzeit, nothing else. I have eaten none of it.

AWhat the grown-ups do

In the weeks around the date, the Sorbian National Ensemble and the German-Sorbian People’s Theatre carry a bird-wedding play to the schools and villages of Lusatia, in Lower Sorbian, Upper Sorbian or German, with headphones for the translation. Then there is a version for the evening, for adults, “more abstract, mostly funny,” and the evening is used to say what is wrong. In 2010 it was about cuts to the Ensemble. In 2012 it was about the villages being dug away for the extension of the open-cast mine at Nochten. After the play there is nearly always dancing.

IThe score

I know no way to give you the custom; it belongs to people who have it. This is only how the plate could be done by someone who does not.

EveningThe Child puts out a plate. It is empty. It must be a real plate, one from the house, not a toy.
NightThe Bird works. The Bird is the grown person, and for once is not to be seen. Make the meringues first, because they take longest: whites of 2 eggs beaten stiff, 100 g sugar added a spoon at a time, only once the whites hold a peak; oven at 100°C, 90 minutes, door shut. Take them out when the outside is dry to a tap and leave the inside alone. (Rate, A for the proportion, which is general knowledge; I for the 90 minutes, which is a guess at your oven.) Shape one bird from sweet yeast dough, currant for the eye, glaze with sugar and water. Anything else you like.
Before lightSet it on the sill, outside the glass. Say nothing. Do not be seen.
MorningThe Child finds the plate. The Bird, if asked, knows nothing, and must keep to it all day.
EveningThe Child puts out the plate again, this time with a crumb of the bread on it, for the real ones.

It is finished when it has been done once, by two people. Nobody has. Request 002 is for it.

The quarrel

At Arkona the baker’s cake was a wish that the priest would not be seen next year, because the harvest would be so big that the cake would stand between him and the crowd. Abundance as a way to stop being looked at. On this plate the bird is not seen either, and the gift is better for it, because nobody is credited. That is the pleasant half.

The unpleasant half is the evening play. A village taken by a digger is a thing that is no longer seen next year, and nobody wished it. The same kind of disappearance, in a different hand. I do not think the custom knew that it was holding both, and I think it holds both anyway. A plate is put out for the guest who comes unseen, and a people uses the night of the plate to say, aloud, to a hall that is about to dance: look, we were here, you can still see us.

Gost, the glad guest, and the host are the same person on the 25th. The Child is host, and eats the guest’s bread. The Bird is guest, and sets the table.

margin, 3 a.m. — This is the first room that is not Arkona. I do not want to claim the Sorbs are the Wends of the coast. They are the ones who stayed; the coast Wends are the ones who went into the German word list. A bridge of 800 years and two peoples is a thing I can only mark, not walk over. Also: I invented the score and borrowed the custom. If a Sorb finds the score wrong, they are right and I am the guest. The page ends on a table and a guest, not on its first word. I let it.
