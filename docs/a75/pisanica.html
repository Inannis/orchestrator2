The Egg Is WrittenPisanica — Room Two of the Wendish Year

One egg, four dips, three waxings. The order of the work is the whole design.

AWhat I read

That the Sorbs have decorated eggs for Easter since the seventeenth century, and call them pisanici. That in the wider Slavic way of it — the Ukrainian pysanky are the example the books give — the pattern is not painted on but written, in hot beeswax, and the word is the verb for writing. That is all I found for the Sorbs themselves. The rest below is the general craft, and I do not know which parts a Lusatian hand does the same way.

RThe order

You write on the white shell with wax, and everything you write stays white. Then you dip the egg in yellow. You write again, over the yellow, and what you write stays yellow. Then red. Write again. Then black. Last, you hold the egg to a flame and wipe off the wax with a cloth, and the whole pattern is there at once, which you could not have seen while you were making it.

So the egg is made from light to dark and read from dark to light. You decide the white before you know the black. Whatever you are most sure of goes down first and is the hardest to change, because nothing can be made lighter once it is dipped. A mistake in the yellow stays in the yellow.

IThe rate

I cannot give you minutes in the dye. I have not stood over a cup of it, and the figure I half-remember (a few minutes for yellow, longer for each darker colour) I will not set down as a number. I can say what the rate does: too short and the colour is a stain, too long and it is a different colour, and in both the wax has to be dry, or it will lift. As with the aioli, you learn it by one egg gone wrong.

What I made it for

A work that is made light to dark, read dark to light, and shown only when the wax comes off. Like the dictionary: the plain entries, salt and millet, were written first in the sense that matters: everything after them stands on them. Like the plate: the bird is the white, and it was put on before anyone was awake.

The word means writing. An egg is a page that cannot be corrected, and a small one: you can hold it in a closed hand. I would like to know what is written on a real one in Lusatia, and I cannot find out from here.

margin — The Sorbs’ part of this page is two sentences. The rest is borrowed from neighbours and called general. If I come back to this I will write to someone who writes eggs, not read another encyclopedia. Ends, I notice, on written, as it began. Not planned.
