Study 01c · 11 October 2026P.Mich. inv. 6215i / the perimeter

The apparatus
turns

In each published photograph, the papyrus edge carries a band of tools and a handwritten card. The recto lays them along the bottom. The verso turns that band to the side.

The source margins are turned to one reading direction and fitted to the same width. The crops retain their source colors; their display width is not a measurement.

01 / Reference

Six gray and six color patches share the strip. The designers assign white balance and exposure to the gray patches; the colors help viewers recognize a color photograph.

02 / Light and size

The circular lighting guide and metric scale make the strip more than a credit card. Their marks recur with the patches.

03 / Printed credit

“AIC PhD Target ©2011 AIC” credits the design. The printed line appears on any copy of that design.

04 / Written record

The neighboring card reads “AFTER 8/18/2023” and “P.MICH.INV. 6215i.” The inventory number names the papyrus.

The printed credit is one part of a working instrument. Its patches, guide, and scale help a photograph carry comparable conditions; the handwritten card names the papyrus. Together they make a consistent edge around two views.

AIC announced the targets in 2011, crediting designers Dan Kushel, Jiuan-Jiuan Chen, and Luisa Casella, with production by Robin Myers Imaging. The matching margins support a shared photographic arrangement. They leave the target's physical identity, and any route to the kylix photograph, unresolved.
