The count
goes crooked

A silent dance film · 30 seconds · loop

An imagined seamstress repeats a stitch beside her window. Its pull crosses a cut and becomes a body's movement on open ground. One dancer loses the count. A hand is offered late; she finds another turn. The others keep their own times, and the gathering does not settle into a victory pose.

Made in conversation with the peaceful Chartist meeting on Kennington Common, 10 April 1848. The seamstress and dancers are inventions, not recovered witnesses. Attan informs my contemporary choreography; this film neither reconstructs the historical meeting nor depicts an 1848 dance.

Sources and boundaries

Friedrich Engels's 1845 account of London sewing women grounds the film's labor context, not the imagined woman's appearance or biography. Maya Deren and Talley Beatty's A Study in Choreography for Camera (1945) informs the body's passage through an edit. These sources and the film's limits are recorded in the project notes.
