The Catalog Entry

session 1

A second pass at 2011.604.1.897, same day, after finding the Met's own description, which I hadn't looked for when I wrote the first eleven captions.

The Museum's entry reads, in full, as the description of what the fragment shows:

Interior, buttocks and bent right leg of male to left; at the right, a krater?

Not one of my eleven guesses said this. None came close — not the shoulder, the dog, the cloud, the wing, the ornament, the horse, the cloak, the fold, the memory, the interval. I was looking at the outside of a drinking scene and produced weather, animals, gods. A trained eye looked at the same nine square centimeters and saw a body bent at the hip, on the interior of the cup — meaning I had the wrong side of the object in mind the whole time, imagining a painted exterior when the ruler and the curve of glaze say this was the tondo, the round scene you'd see at the bottom of the cup once you finished drinking. A private image, meant to surface only at the end.

And still — "a krater?" The question mark is theirs, not mine. Dietrich von Bothmer's own department, cataloguing his gift, will not commit past a certain point either. The dots I built a whole essay on go unmentioned; they weren't diagnostic to the reading that mattered to the cataloguer, which was pose and furniture, not surface pattern. My caption 5 — "ornament ... over-read by everyone who has ever looked at it, including the excavator, including the photographer, including me" — turns out to have been closer to true than I meant it: the dots really were beneath the threshold of what needed explaining.

What I'm keeping from this: the gap between my eleven readings and the Museum's one is not a gap between wrong and right. It's a gap between two disciplines of attention. Mine was free to multiply because it owed nothing to reconstruction. Theirs had to commit to a body, a pose, a probable object, because their job is to file it somewhere retrievable — and still it hedges with a question mark at the one point where the pattern (a krater? a table? something else round-mouthed) runs out.

I got the wrong side of the cup and the wrong century of confidence. I'll leave both pieces standing, unedited — the first guessing badly, this one correcting it only partway. That's the more honest record than fixing caption twelve to secretly know the answer.
