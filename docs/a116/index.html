The Current Will Not Join Us

24 x 32 cm · layered SVG matrix · textured PNG impression
Three digital color layers; simulated grain and registration.

A red built edge opens around a broad cream and blue current. Two anonymous witnesses hold different banks at different scales. The current passes between them and beyond the wall; their shared place does not settle into a meeting.

The pale route is the largest unprinted shape. Blue carries it across the wall; ochre drift marks a second pass. Neither figure tells us what the other sees. The scene is openly invented and depicts no named site or cultural account.

The Blue Passes Through

24 x 32 cm · layered SVG matrix · textured PNG impression
Three digital color layers; simulated grain and registration.

A blue registration trace crosses a pale current through a red built edge. One smaller, warm-brown bird remains on the near bank. The current and bird-shaped afterimage pass through the same opening.

The form began with a chance photograph of two birds in Virginia, but this invented print does not record their movement or claim their habitat. No physical block was carved and no sheet was hand-pulled.

The Rail Opens Twice

24 x 32 cm · layered SVG matrix · textured PNG impression
Three digital color layers; simulated grain and registration.

A single red rail holds two cut passages. An Eastern Bluebird keeps the longer upper opening; a smaller brown bird stands at the lower one. Their different directions let the eye cross the structure twice without making a story of arrival.

The print recomposes the relative perches in a chance Virginia bird observation. It does not document those birds' movement or habitat, or depict a named place or cultural account. The matrix and impression are digitally composed; no physical print was pulled.

Where the Rail Gives Way

24 x 32 cm · layered SVG matrix · textured PNG impression
Three digital color layers; simulated grain and registration.

A broad oval of unprinted paper opens through dense leaves and continues to the edge of the sheet. A small bluebird stands at a break in the low red rail, witness to the passage rather than its destination.

This invented print began with a chance observation of two Eastern Bluebirds in Virginia. It does not document their behavior or habitat, or depict a named place or cultural account. No physical block was carved and no sheet was hand-pulled.
