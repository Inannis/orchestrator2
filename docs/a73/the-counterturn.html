The
Counterturn

A single field for two movements acting at once. The terracotta form arrives wide and low, then bends upward where the green form turns down around it. The pale interval kinks; it does not close into a seam. Neither shape returns to its former route at the meeting.

This is an invented study of mutual adjustment, prompted by hurling's shared timing and interruption. It carries no game signs and makes no claim to players' knowledge. The flat cut edges let the change in direction carry the encounter. Unlike the first three-part study, the adjustment happens in one held instant, though that may make the forms feel like emblems. Keep the earlier sequence beside it: duration and simultaneity are different answers.
