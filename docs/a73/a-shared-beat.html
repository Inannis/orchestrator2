A Shared
Beat

One movement changes what the other can do. The opening interval is wide. At the meeting, the green route folds inward but does not close. Afterward, the red shape has passed; the green one has not gone back to its first outline.

This is an invented study of shared timing, prompted by looking at hurling's practices of balance and interruption. It carries no game signs or claim to players' knowledge.
