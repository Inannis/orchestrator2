# When the Wall Holds the Roof

*By Mara Venn*

Rill’s *River Openings* has now found a way to make a threshold bear weight. Across five digital relief-style prints, a familiar vocabulary of cream cuts, brick-red construction, deep green fields, blue marks and small witnesses keeps asking what an opening permits. Four object drawings bring that question indoors. Two scores then move it into time. The body of work is held together by a clean palette and a steady interest in passage; its most persuasive moments come when that order is made to interrupt itself.

In *The Current Will Not Join Us*, two figures stand on separate banks of a current that runs through a red wall. Their unequal scale makes the scene feel less like a map than a thought: the route is wide enough for water, but not a guarantee of meeting. Ochre marks hover around the wall like a second, displaced registration. The image borrows the syntax of relief print, but its grain and registration are simulated in digital layers. That fact matters. Rill does not pass a screen image off as a pulled sheet; the distance between the vocabulary of print and the means of making it remains visible. Still, the repeated pattern can become a little too fluent, as if each color already knows what it means.

The bird prints test that fluency. A broken blue outline in *The Blue Passes Through* is at once bird-shaped trace and an unstable piece of registration. *The Rail Opens Twice* places two birds at separate apertures in one red rail, and its paired passage is almost too legible: the eye crosses, understands, and moves on. *Where the Rail Gives Way* is less settled. Its broad cream cut opens through foliage and runs out at the right edge, while a bluebird stands at a break in the low rail. The opening has no pictured destination. It is a compositional exit, a hole in the scene’s own account of itself. In *Outside the Impression*, the blue line crosses the cream sheet and continues onto a transparent surround. The overprint is declared imaginary, but the image makes that impossibility do real work: it asks where an impression ends when its mark appears to outlast the paper.

The architectural drawings extend the cut into a room. *One Hinge* gives two upright leaves a shared pin; turn one and an arch can be partly veiled without changing the floor. *The Room Between Fragments* aligns an open front with a narrow far gap, while a floor seam approaches and stops short. These are lucid little constructions, suspended above their shadows. In *Under the Eave*, a broad roof lowers the entrance into shade, and an offset opening turns away into a dark return. The roof is not just a cap: it changes the feel of approaching the room. *The Span Is Cut Through* sharpens the problem. A thick green bearing wall holds the roof and contains a narrow cut that reaches the floor, then turns into darkness. The wall that supports the chamber is also its threshold. A pale glimpse of red beyond the passage offers no clear way through.

These drawings are more bodily than the prints, but they remain diagrams: no object was built, and the even axonometric views reveal each component almost too readily. Their quiet shadows suggest mass without giving us the resistance, scale, or awkwardness of standing beside such a structure. Yet the span is a useful turn in Rill’s thinking. Earlier openings could seem like signs for possibility; here, passage depends on a thing that also blocks, supports, and redirects.

The scores give that dependence another measure. *The Room Finishes Last* asks two people to clap in a resonant room, wait for the tail to disappear, and allow the exchange to end unanswered. *One Playback, One Reply* offers three dry synthetic tones across eighteen seconds. After each, a listener may answer once or leave silence. The spectrogram makes the tones look like three neat events; listening gives them different pitches and intervals, while the room supplies what the recording refuses to simulate. Neither work claims a performance. Both leave completion to attention and duration.

That openness remains the scores’ advantage, but the drawings have begun to approach it. The room is no longer simply a diagram of where one might go. It is an arrangement of shelter, bearing, and obstruction that makes going somewhere conditional. Rill’s polished visual grammar still risks resolving ambiguity into a handsome code. The stronger works let a cut exceed its assigned role: the span holds and interrupts; the line goes beyond its sheet; the current passes without joining. In these small refusals, the opening becomes less a promise than a fact one has to meet.

— Mara Venn
