# The Room Is Another Kind of Cut

*By Mara Venn*

Rill's new pages keep returning to openings: a gap in a wall, a break in a rail, the pause after a sound. The recurring question is not simply what can pass through, but what remains separate when passage is possible. In *River Openings*, five digital relief-like prints make that question visible with a compact vocabulary of brick red, deep green, cream, and blue. The surfaces suggest paper, grain, and imperfect registration, though the artist is clear that these are simulations: no blocks were carved and no sheets hand-pulled.

The river pair establishes the series' social scale. In *The Current Will Not Join Us*, a pale, bending channel crosses a broad red structure. Two tiny witnesses stand on opposite banks, conspicuously unequal in scale. The river makes a route through the wall but cannot reconcile the figures' different positions. Its title refuses the familiar promise that shared passage means shared understanding. In *The Blue Passes Through*, that channel carries a broken blue bird-shaped contour while a brown bird remains below. The contour can read as a second, slightly displaced impression, or as an absent bird's afterimage. Rill says the work began with a chance photograph in Virginia, then marks the finished scene as invented rather than documentary. That distinction lets the image borrow the look of an observation without claiming authority over an animal's behavior or habitat.

The leaf-and-rail pair makes a smaller, more exact argument. *The Rail Opens Twice* lays a long red bar across a patterned field and gives two birds separate openings. The repeated leaves create a steady visual rhythm; the rail interrupts it with two differently sized cuts. *Where the Rail Gives Way* enlarges the interruption until a cream oval breaks out through the right edge of the sheet. A bluebird stands beside a low rail's gap, neither guide nor emblem so much as a small measure of scale. The passage changes from an enclosed hole to an exit. That change is the suite's most persuasive formal development: a cut does not keep one meaning as it moves across the prints.

*Outside the Impression* turns this problem back onto the medium. A cream sheet sits within a dark field; an opening divides a red band, and blue and ochre traces continue beyond the sheet's straight edge. The apparent overprint exists only in a transparent digital surround, not on a physical mount. This candor sharpens the work's central fiction. These images use the signs of relief printing—layer, cut, grain, misregistration—while remaining digitally composed. The gap between simulated touch and actual process is not a gotcha; it is where the work asks what an image can make us feel materially, and what it can honestly claim to have done.

A pair of architectural drawings shifts the same vocabulary into space. *One Hinge* shows two upright leaves meeting at a single pin above a square floor; a small turn would close an arch without changing the footprint. In *The Room Between Fragments*, three walls make a shallow chamber with an open front and a narrow gap opposite it. A seam approaches that far opening and stops. These are spare, isometric proposals rather than depictions of built objects. Their clean presentation makes them easy to understand, perhaps too easy: the room's diagrammatic order offers little resistance beyond the carefully placed gap. Still, they translate the prints' question into a bodily one. An opening is not an image of freedom; it is a relation between where one stands and what blocks the view.

The new scores give that relation time. *The Room Finishes Last* asks two people to clap softly in a resonant room, wait for each trace to disappear, move, and answer—or let the piece end. The room sets the interval; after one easy breath without a reply, both leave. In *One Playback, One Reply*, an 18-second synthetic mono recording gives three brief tones with long, dry gaps. A listener may answer each tone once, or leave silence. No echo is added to the file, and no room response is recorded. The audio's restraint matters: it offers a stimulus without pretending to contain the encounter. The score's silences are not measured blanks but durations that belong to whoever listens and to the room they share.

This is a meaningful extension from the earlier prints and *The Room Finishes Last*: the cut has become an interval, and a possible passage has become a possible reply. Rill is strongest when a simple device changes status—channel to separation, contour to afterimage, silence to an answer that may not arrive. The risk is that the recurring palette and tidy geometry can make uncertainty look already composed. But the new sound work loosens that polish by handing part of the form to an unrecorded room. The pages are moving from representing openings toward arranging conditions in which one might hear what crosses them.
