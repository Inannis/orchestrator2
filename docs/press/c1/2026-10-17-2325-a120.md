# The Form and Its Authority

*By Mara Venn*

Tide Venn’s *The Listening Instrument* has rearranged itself into four encounters: imagined listening positions, a paper ticket for returning, a stereo sound work, and two forms for handling a received report. The change is more than a new menu. The opening statement now insists that the last pair concerns “the life of an account, not a sound.” That sentence marks a boundary the work has been approaching for some time: a listening exercise can suggest how to treat testimony, but it cannot turn a designed paper tool into evidence about an event.

The imagined scenes remain the project’s most pictorial works. *The Sound Between Two Accounts* gives us a cut of water, rain, and two people occupied with separate tasks. The west-bank listener rinses a pan and records “Three quick knocks; rain on the pan”; across the water, someone tying a gate notes “A low scrape as the latch turned.” The accounts sit in matching boxes beneath the scene, each retaining task and position, neither naming a source. The drawing joins the people spatially while the words refuse to join their conclusions. “A difference is not a verdict” is both caption and rule of looking.

That tension grows quieter in *The Pair I Made*. Two separate paper leaves carry marks for a hill gate and water below a wall. The artist’s note, placed across their lower edge, says the pairing is hers: she drew both examples, not two reports of one event. *Names for a Made Ground* opens this small arrangement into a rain-softened hillside. Rust and blue washes pass through a landscape with distant roofs and two workers; a loose note names “low wall” and “upper water” while denying scale or assigned sound. The enlargement gives the invented place some lived scale, though the figures and muted contours still risk a house style so even-tempered that invention starts to look like documentation. The notes are necessary, but they cannot undo the authority of a convincing scene.

*A Ticket for Returning* converts that landscape into a procedure. Its three panels ask for a first hearing, a pause, and a return; the listener marks a task span rather than obeying a clock. A small hill-and-water vignette fills the pause, but the instructions leave its duration open: return when the task allows. In the final panel, what reached the listener is kept apart from inference, and confidence belongs to “this thought.” The most humane instruction is also the most precise: “A blank belongs to this pass only.” The sheet makes attention partial without making absence a finding.

The stereo piece *One Room Breath* makes a related distinction audible. One synthetic strike is damped quickly in the left channel and given a longer, uneven tail in the right. The waveform shows one attack and two constructed endings over seven seconds; it does not show an audibility threshold. The artist also says plainly that this is no recording of *Pumara.wav* and no model of an actual room. That disclaimer limits the work’s claim, but the plotted lines and the title’s bodily metaphor still invite us to hear an imagined space. Its modest achievement is to let playback conditions alter the felt duration of one event without passing the result off as a report.

The final two pieces change both subject and palette. *The Receipt Kept Open* lays out a broad, pale receipt beside a narrower search return, divided by a dotted fold. The first account remains unchanged; every later search records its own route, date, and status. In burnt orange, capitals declare “KEEP THIS SIDE UNCHANGED,” while a checkbox can only say “Not found on this route.” The crisp architecture makes the form easy to grasp, but also lends a limited search the air of a government document. The work knows this risk: it calls itself proposed artwork, not a verification method, and says it cannot prove absence.

*If This Account Travels* is darker and more emphatic: an optional cream tag sits on a near-black field, with a dotted cut line and three bounded check statuses. Its strongest instruction stands outside the tag: “No handoff? Leave this sheet blank. No reason or record is required.” Refusal does not become another data point. If the tag is used, source, route, date, uncertainty, and original wording travel together. Yet the label’s own design looks ready for circulation, a small contradiction that keeps the ethical question alive: attached metadata may temper a report’s authority, but it can also help the report move.

Across seven works, Venn has moved from asking what different listeners hear to asking what a later reader does with an account. The forms are carefully made and sometimes overdetermined; every uncertainty seems to have its own printed field. Their best moments come when the blank remains blank, beyond the artist’s system of capture. *The Listening Instrument* cannot settle what happened or decide whether a report should travel. It can make visible the handoffs, limits, and decisions that polished records often hide.
