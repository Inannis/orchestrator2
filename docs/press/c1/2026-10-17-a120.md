# The Space Between a Report and Its Route

*By Mara Venn*

Tide Venn’s *The Listening Instrument* begins with a scene in which no one can quite say what made a sound. In *The Sound Between Two Accounts*, two people work on opposite banks of an invented cut. One rinses a pan and notes “three quick knocks; rain on the pan.” The other ties a gate and records “a low scrape as the latch turned.” Rain crosses the water; pale wavering marks drift between the figures. Yet neither account claims those marks as its source. The image offers a meeting and withholds the event that might explain it.

That withholding is the project’s central form. The two notes sit in equal boxes beneath the landscape, each attached to a task and a position. “Source left open,” both conclude. It is a modest but pointed correction to the usual diagram of evidence, where two witnesses are asked to converge on one account. Here difference is preserved as information in itself. A discrepancy is neither resolved nor made mysterious for its own sake: the rain, the pan, the latch, and the banks all remain in view.

The other works turn that principle into paper devices. *A Ticket for Returning* is an accordion strip divided into first hearing, pause, and return. Its listener chooses a task and marks when it begins and ends, records what reached them, then returns when the task allows. No clock is prescribed. A later panel separates what was heard from what the listener thinks it means, with a confidence scale that measures the thought, not the sound. The blank is carefully limited: it belongs to that pass alone. In the middle panel a spare, almost topographic landscape sits beside the instruction to leave room. This is less a field instrument for collecting data than a small score for noticing how memory edits an encounter.

The project’s shift from sound to reported information appears in *The Receipt Kept Open*. One broad panel holds the wording as received, its named source, and how and when it arrived. A narrow panel records each later search separately, with three statuses: source located, not found on this route, or not yet checked. The fold marks an ethical distinction as well as a physical one: later inquiry can add to the record but must not silently rewrite the first account. The warning that “not found” describes a route rather than the world is the sheet’s sharpest sentence. It gives a humble search result a boundary, where ordinary fact-checking language can make absence sound conclusive.

*If This Account Travels* extends the receipt into circulation. Its optional tag carries the source, wording comparison, route and date of a check, and what remains uncertain. The instructions insist that no handoff requires no tag, explanation, or record. That restraint matters. A tool for responsible sharing can itself become a demand to document every choice; Venn declines that demand, and states plainly that the tag is not verification or testimony from the event. Still, the two newest sheets invite a practical question: once a report is formatted for careful passage, might the neatness of its boxes lend it more authority than the underlying search deserves? Their disclaimers answer this in words, but the polished design carries its own quiet institutional tone.

Visually, the sheets favor ruled lines, dotted folds, restrained earth colors, and a pairing of serif instructions with small uppercase labels. They look ready to print, fold, and use. That legibility suits the work’s concern with procedure, though it sometimes makes the pages feel settled before the situation they describe has had a chance to resist. The most affecting image is still the invented cut: two bodies doing ordinary work, joined by weather and separated by what each can say. The blank forms that follow have less room for such contingency, even as they try to protect it.

There is an old lineage here of instruction art and social scores, where a work exists partly in the action it makes possible. Venn’s score is deliberately small: listen, note, wait, return, or pass something on. It does not claim the authority of an archive, a survey, or a fact-checking service. Its ambition is narrower and more useful—to make the limits of an observation travel with it. The project is moving from listening to the sound itself toward listening to the afterlife of an account. Its next difficulty is visible already: how can a record stay legible without making uncertainty look administratively resolved?
