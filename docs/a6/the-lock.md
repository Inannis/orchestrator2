A cartographer in Lisbon once redrew the coastline every decade because the sea refused to hold still. Her apprentice measured tide pools with a brass compass inherited from a grandfather who distrusted maps entirely. Meanwhile a baker three streets over timed his ovens by the sound gulls made at dawn, a method nobody could explain but nobody could disprove either. Trains from the north carried crates of oranges that smelled faintly of diesel by the time they reached the market. A retired clockmaker fixed watches for free on Thursdays, muttering about gear ratios to customers who only wanted the time to be correct again. Children chased a kite shaped like a heron over the docks until the wind dropped and the heron folded itself onto the cobblestones. A violinist practiced scales behind a shuttered window, unaware that a sailor two floors below had stopped packing his bag just to listen. The harbor authority repainted the lighthouse a duller white than before, and nobody wrote in to complain, which the harbormaster found more unsettling than complaints would have been. A librarian catalogued shipping manifests from a century earlier, finding a recipe for preserved lemons tucked between two invoices. Fishermen argued about currents the way other people argue about politics, with the same stubbornness and roughly the same evidence. A stray dog adopted the fish market as its territory and the vendors adopted it back, feeding it scraps in exchange for chasing off gulls. Somewhere inland, a vineyard owner worried about frost that hadn't arrived yet and might not arrive at all this year. A photographer visiting from abroad kept missing the exact moment the light turned gold, arriving each evening a few minutes too late. Two old friends argued cheerfully over dominoes outside a cafe that had changed owners four times but kept the same chairs. A customs officer stamped a passport without looking up, thinking instead about a letter he hadn't finished writing to his sister. The tide came in exactly as the cartographer's apprentice had predicted, though nobody except her seemed to notice or care that the prediction had been right.
