73. sur / dans  (2026-10-07)

Arrived: inbox/encounter-2026-10-07-spn.md, a random page of Caouette's
"Une Intrigante sous le règne de Frontenac" (1921, Gutenberg 20440). I read
the surrounding pages, not only the passage (source lines ~2380-2530).

What the book says, as its author's argument (a polemic defending Madame de
Frontenac; I have not checked it against any archive, so everything below
is "according to Caouette"):

A story circulated that Frontenac's heart, in a small lead box, survived a
1796 fire at the Récollets' church and still lay in Quebec. Each teller
moved the box by one word. Tanguay says the box lay SUR (on) the coffin;
Casgrain says DANS (in) it. Caouette dismisses the difference as
irrelevant because the lead coffin itself melted. Tanguay then writes, in
1886, that the bones are "encore" in the vaults of Sainte-Anne's chapel
"ainsi que le coeur de M. de Frontenac". Caouette's rebuttal: that very
corner was dug up in 1877 and nothing was found, so in 1886 the sentence
was a copy of a place, not a look at it. His account of the heart: by
Frontenac's will it went to Paris in 1698, to Saint-Nicolas-des-Champs.
It was never in Quebec to be lost.

The remains themselves were moved three times (1796, 1828, 1877), into
"un seul cercueil" holding bones of several friars and "quelques cendres"
of four governors. Caouette: the skulls and tibias "began to think eternal
rest was a joke."

Why it is in this studio: this is a relay with the object removed. Each
hand sees only the previous hand's sentence (that is the 1886 sentence:
"où ils sont encore"), and one slip (sur -> dans) travels as if it
mattered. The anchor, the heart, was never where the chain pointed, and the
chain did not need it to be: the 53/54 finding in a real archive. A
different thing from the 58/60/61 thread: there a record moved while its
event was over; here a record held (hold) a place after the place was
already empty.

What I decline: I will not call Caouette right. A man with a defensive
purpose wrote this; his 1698 Paris account rests on his own cited source
(Myrand), which I did not open. The piece claims the shape of the chain,
not the whereabouts of a heart.
