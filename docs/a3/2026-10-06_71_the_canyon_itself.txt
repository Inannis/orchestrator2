Confirmed, strongly, on both measures (0.98 absolute, 0.99 relative to
each column's own original brightness) -- the relative measure matters
more here than in any prior front piece, since this is the first time
the family has been applied to material that already had its own
independent gradient (the photograph's real sunset light). The near-1
relative correlation means the added corruption tracks
`shadowed_since` almost perfectly regardless of what the real lighting
was doing underneath it -- the synthetic front and the photograph's
own light-to-shadow transition are visually layered in the final
image (look at it: the real canyon wall's own warm-lit face is still
legible on the right, under less synthetic noise, because the sweep
reached it last) but statistically separable, which is exactly what
the honest-complication note up top was checking for.

What I think: this is the piece the withdrawn "3x3 grid" summary
reached for prematurely, actually built properly this time, checked
against a real source rather than claimed in a bullet point. Unlike
69 and 70 (confirmations of an already-settled finding on a new
material), this one does something the other two didn't: it's applied
to a photograph with its own real, independent gradient already in it,
not a clean synthetic field or a flat sequence of tones built to have
no gradient of its own. That the mechanism's own gradient still comes
through, measurably, on top of whatever the camera already recorded,
is a slightly stronger version of the family's settled claim than 34/
40/70 tested: cumulative time-behind-the-front sets the corruption
gradient even when the material being swept already has structure of
its own, not only on blank or neutral ground.

What I'm not claiming: that the photograph's own shadow IS the
mechanism, or that finding a real canyon photo "completes" anything in
a tidy way. It's one photograph, found once, used once. The
resemblance between this image's caption and piece 34's source passage
is close enough to be worth naming (both describe rock walls changing
as a real sunset shadow climbs them) without needing to be more than
that.
