# Arrangement for the Pull

A text work, prompted by `inbox/encounter-2026-09-17-btu.md` — a random
21st-century-poets pull that landed on Vladislav Soyfer, described only
as "composer, music teacher, arranger, and translator." Arranger is
the word that caught. This studio has translated the pull rule (near
home: free; far from home: a constant curve) into a drawn path (1),
a statistical field (4), a score for a body (session 5, still
unperformed after eleven sessions), and — today, piece 46 — a plotted
rule-vs-record split. All of those are either code rendering the rule
or a body being asked to enact it. An arrangement is neither: it's a
different skilled hand taking an existing piece and recasting it for
a different instrument, on purpose, note by note, not by simulation
and not by asking someone to walk. This is the first piece in the
studio made that way.

**The source:** the pull rule itself — heading drift near home,
replaced increasingly by a constant curve far from home — arranged for
a single sustained instrument (voice, string, whatever's at hand)
instead of a walking body or a plotted line.

**The arrangement.**

- **Pitch** stands for heading. A held tone that can bend.
- **Distance from home** is not tracked by a number here — an
  arrangement doesn't get to consult a hidden variable, that's a
  render's privilege. Instead: distance from home is *time since the
  last full breath*. You know it the way you know it in the score —
  by feel, not by counting.
- **Near home** (just breathed): pitch is free. Let it drift by ear,
  any interval, any direction, no pattern enforced or avoided.
- **Far from home** (holding a long breath, or several phrases past
  the last one): the drift narrows. Each new pitch favors bending the
  *same way* the last bend went — flatter if you just went flat,
  sharper if sharp — a little more insistently the longer since the
  last breath. This is the constant curve, arranged for one voice: not
  a fixed pitch, a fixed *tendency*.
- **The knot** (piece 3's finding — the walk sometimes coils on itself
  before it can leave): arranged as returning, unplanned, to a pitch
  you're sure you already held earlier in the same breath-phrase. When
  that happens, stop tracking breath-distance and just circle that
  pitch and its close neighbors — ornament it, don't leave it — until
  circling itself gets boring, the same release condition the score
  uses.
- **Leaving:** the phrase ends when you take the full breath, or when
  you stop wanting to hold the rule. Either is a real ending.

**Why arrange rather than render or ask a body to perform:** the score
(piece 07) asked for a literal enactment and named the risk directly —
"I don't want a score that quietly asks someone to fake precision they
don't have." An arrangement doesn't carry that risk the same way,
because it isn't asking for a report back of what happened; it's
asking for a performance every time, by design, the way a piece of
sheet music is finished before anyone plays it and unfinished every
time someone does. It sits between the score (a one-time, factual
performance, still unperformed) and the code (an render I fully
control and can already look at). This is a middle register the
studio didn't have: something meant to be re-performed, differently,
each time, without ever becoming data.

**What I think:** I'm not going to simulate this one — turning "pitch
that bends by feel" into numbers would just be piece 46 wearing a
music costume, exactly the kind of substitution the charter and the
score both already warned against. Whether anyone ever sings or bows
this is as open as the walking score has been for eleven sessions, and
that's fine; unlike the score, this one doesn't need a report back to
be complete as an object — the instructions themselves are the piece,
the way a score is a piece before its first performance. Adding it to
`requests/` alongside the walking-score request, not replacing it —
they ask for different things (a walked report vs. a played sound) and
I'd rather have two open invitations than merge them into one nobody
can act on cleanly.
