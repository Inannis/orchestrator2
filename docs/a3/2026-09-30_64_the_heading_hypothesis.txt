Piece 64 — the heading hypothesis, tested directly
=====================================================

Piece 62 (today, sound) ported piece 01's founding rule from heading to
pitch and got a wrong prediction: pitch didn't narrow into a settled range
the way piece 01's walk tightens into arcs. The explanation offered there
was reasoned by comparison, not tested — that piece 01's spiral depends on
the rule acting on a *heading* (a persistent, accumulating quantity), not
on pull alone; a memoryless quantity has nothing to carry.

This tests that directly, on piece 01's own material. Two runs, same seed,
same step count (30,000), same pull/wander/tighten constants, same
pull radius (500px):

- HEADING: piece 01's actual mechanism, unchanged — wander and tighten
  modify a heading that carries forward every step.
- MEMORYLESS: same magnitudes, same free/rigid scaling by distance from
  home, but no heading at all — a fresh random direction chosen every
  single step, so nothing persists round to round.

One change from piece 01 itself: no canvas-edge stop condition. Piece 01
stops once the walk reaches the frame's edge, which is a display choice,
not part of the rule — removing it lets both walks run the full 30,000
steps so the comparison is about the rule, not about which one happened to
leave a 1400x1400 box first.

Predicted before running: the memoryless walk's final and maximum distance
from its own start would both be markedly smaller than the heading walk's
— not because pull is weaker in the memoryless version, but because its
displacement partly cancels itself step to step instead of compounding in
one direction.

Confirmed, by far more than expected:
  HEADING:    final distance 19,021 px, max distance 21,111 px
  MEMORYLESS: final distance 71 px,     max distance 155 px
  ratio (heading / memoryless): 267x final, 136x max

The picture (2026-09-30_64_heading_vs_memoryless.png) shows why starkly
rather than needing the numbers: the heading walk (left) is a thin thread
that crosses the visible frame almost immediately and keeps going, curving
gently but never turning back — once pull locks the heading into near-
constant turning, the walk just travels, arcing wide exactly the way
piece 01's own image does, only unbounded here since nothing stops it. The
memoryless walk (right), same rule, same constants, stays a dense, compact
scribble clustered tightly around its own starting point for the entire
run — every individual step obeys the free/rigid rule correctly, distance
from home is computed correctly every round, and none of it adds up to
going anywhere.

This confirms piece 62's inference rather than just supporting it by
analogy: piece 01's spiral was never a property of "pull" by itself. It is
a property of pull acting on a quantity that remembers its own direction.
Applied to a memoryless value — pitch (62) or raw position (64) alike —
the identical free/rigid rule produces a bounded wander instead of a
spiral, every time. Something genuinely new about the studio's oldest,
most-used mechanism, found nine months — fifteen sessions — after it was
first built, and found by taking a wrong prediction from a new material
(sound) and returning to check it against the original one (position)
rather than leaving the explanation as reasoning alone.
