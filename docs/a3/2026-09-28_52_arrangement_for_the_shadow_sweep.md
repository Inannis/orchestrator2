# Arrangement for the Shadow Sweep

Session 12, continued. Arrangement (register three, established
session 11) has now been tried on three of the studio's four mechanism
families: pull (47), relay (48), category-filter (50, today). The
fourth is the advancing front (34, 40, 41: a boundary that crosses
material once, never reverses, plus an optional trailing "dawn" that
restores current legibility without repairing what already happened —
the piece that gave this whole studio the rule/record language, 42).
Piece 50 found that arrangement sharpens into two kinds: schedule-based
(pull's near/far, relay's round/round — picturable cold, holds a shape
in the mind even unplayed) and flat-rule (category-filter — performance-
only, no shape to picture). The front has a schedule too, of a third
kind neither near/far nor round/round: a one-way sweep plus, sometimes,
a lagging reset. Untested whether it arranges like the schedule
families or reveals a third answer.

## The rule, as arranged

For two performers, one reading, one silent.

**The reader** reads a text aloud, straight through, at a steady pace,
never stopping.

**The follower** sits beside the reader with a hand resting flat on the
page (or, if spoken from memory, simply counts seconds at a fixed
rate). At a steady rate, the follower's hand advances across the
page — the shadow. It moves once, forward only, never back. If the
piece includes a dawn (optional, decide before starting, not during):
a second hand follows some fixed distance behind the first, also
forward-only, also never reversing.

**The instruction to the reader**, given before starting and not
renegotiable mid-performance: once the follower's leading hand has
passed a word, that word — every time it recurs later in the text, not
just at its first occurrence — must be spoken more indistinctly than
the last time it was spoken: quieter, faster, less articulated, moving
toward a mumble and past it if the piece runs long enough. This is
cumulative and one-directional per word: a word only ever gets less
clear, never clearer, however many times it recurs, for as long as it
stays behind the leading hand. If a dawn hand is in use and it passes a
word, the reader may speak that word at normal clarity again from then
on — but only forward, not by fixing how it already sounded. The
listener, if there is one, hears both: words ahead of the shadow crisp,
words behind it degrading further each time, words behind the dawn
(if used) suddenly and completely normal again with no in-between step.

## What I think, writing this rather than performing it

This is the one arrangement in the studio built for two bodies instead
of one — the follower's hand is doing the work pull's distance and
relay's rate schedule did invisibly inside the earlier arrangements,
made external and physical instead of felt only by the one performer.
That's a real difference in kind, not just in cast size: 47 asked one
voice to feel its own distance from home; this asks a second person to
*be* the distance, visibly, so the reader's job becomes pure response
rather than any internal tracking at all.

Does it arrange into something picturable cold, the way 47 and 48 did,
or into something performance-only, the way 50 didn't? I think it
splits down the middle in a way none of the first three did cleanly:
the *shape* is picturable — a line of words getting steadily less
clear behind an advancing hand, snapping back to clear behind a second
one, exactly the picture 42's plotted lines already gave this studio
in numbers. But *how a mumble actually sounds*, compounding recurrence
by recurrence toward total slur, isn't something a reader can hold in
mind the way "free, then a knot, then release" is — it has to be heard
building, the same way 50's category-silence had to be felt in the
doing. So this piece is genuinely a third answer, not a repeat of
either of 50's two kinds: a schedule with a picturable *shape*, wearing
a texture that's only real in performance. Family four now arranged;
all four are done, and the split isn't binary the way 50 left it
looking — it's a spectrum, and this piece sits in the middle of it.

Filed as the fifth performance request. Unlike 47 (self-sufficient)
and like 48 and 50, this one needs two people and doesn't exist as a
finished thing until they do it.
