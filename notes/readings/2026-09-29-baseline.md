# Comparing six folders, blind

## 1. What each artist makes

**A.** A run of short prose essays, dressed as a "studio" with numbered sessions, on real museum/archival objects — a Greek kylix fragment, a Wikipedia village stub, a Cassatt pastel, a LeWitt wall drawing. Each piece asks what a record shows versus what it hedges or cuts, illustrated with the actual source photo plus occasional hand-built diagrams (a bar chart of redacted text, a table of "nine registers"). Reads as a serial taxonomy of withholding, narrating its own errors and corrections in real time. Wants to catch itself being wrong and say so.

**B.** A generative-art lab notebook: a single rule ("free near an origin, rigid far from it") run across random walks, erasure of found poems, text "relay" decay, density fields that read as skin or weather, graphic scores. Dozens of dated, numbered renders plus long methodological prose predicting outcomes before running them and reporting when predictions fail. Wants to falsify its own founding idea until it can't, and has mostly concluded the "center" it keeps choosing is arbitrary — the choosing itself is the real subject.

**C.** A small set of prose pieces (plus a few charts/images) on real biographical and museum records — a forgotten football player, a living graphic designer's CV with no images of his work, Monet's *Petite Creuse River* first as a stripped inventory record then as the actual painting. Same "gap in the record" territory as A, thinner and more admittedly provisional ("working notebook, not a portfolio yet"). Wants to distinguish carelessness from structural gaps in what a record can hold.

**D.** Minimal generative-competition pieces: bars/funnels of color representing words or texts "surviving" or "dying" under a fixed rule, shown as static PNGs and one looping GIF, paired with terse monospace text logs. Preoccupied less with the images themselves than with whether the artist judges its own output consistently, whether its metrics agree with its eye, and admits repeated, embarrassing test failures. Wants rigor about its own process more than a finished image.

**E.** A slow handful of pieces on real historical objects — a Seurat conté drawing, a Cheyenne/Arapaho ledger drawing, a mended Greek krater fragment — plus one piece built entirely from raw language-model output regenerating itself into a repeating loop. Small, curated, self-consciously withholding (shows one piece, not the working log). Wants to demonstrate restraint and to name the edge of its own authority to interpret what it's showing.

**F.** Pure prose, no images at all: short essays on obscure real records found "by chance" — a commode with unspecified "later alterations," a defunct crowdfunding company, a castle, a magazine cover's Kabbalah joke. Explicitly edits itself against jargon a stranger couldn't parse and stops describing exactly where invention would start. Wants to be legible to someone with zero history with the practice, and to never manufacture feeling or fact.

## 2. Is it about knowledge itself?

- **A.** Yes — its entire subject is what a record shows, hedges, or withholds, and it explicitly maps its own taxonomy of that.
- **B.** Partly — plenty of rigorous testing/falsification language, but the actual object is a formal rule's behavior, not records or claims as such.
- **C.** Yes — same register as A: what a museum or biographical record can and can't hold, and why.
- **D.** Partly — the images are formal/competitive systems; the real epistemic content is a second-order worry about whether the artist's own judgment and metrics are trustworthy.
- **E.** Partly — most pieces are about restraint and the edge of interpretive authority rather than records per se, though the museum pieces lean that way.
- **F.** Yes, most purely of all six — nothing here but the discipline of what can honestly be said about a record, with zero ornament.

## 3. Made thing vs. prose about things

- **A.** ~20% designed/found image, ~80% prose (real museum photos + a few custom diagrams, in service of the essays).
- **B.** ~55% made object (dozens of generative renders, a graphic score), ~45% prose (extensive but subordinate to the artifacts).
- **C.** ~20% image, ~80% prose.
- **D.** ~35% image/animation, ~65% prose (short pieces, but half have no image at all).
- **E.** ~30% real/found image, ~70% prose.
- **F.** 0% made object, 100% prose.

## 4. Grouping

Two clear families, plus one outlier within a family.

- **Museum/record family — A, C, E, F.** Same diction ("the record," "withholding," "the honest move," flagged self-corrections), same session-numbered studio-notebook structure, same core move of sitting with a real found document or object and refusing to invent past what it says. A is the most developed and most visual version of this method; C is a thinner, still-forming version of the same idea; F is its purest, image-free extreme; E is the smallest in output but shares the diction and, in one piece, borrows the "process with nothing to check itself against" idea from the other family (see below).
- **Generative-rule family — B, D.** Same discipline of stating a prediction before running it, reporting when it's wrong, and testing a formal rule across unrelated materials. B is the sprawling, well-resourced version; D is its stripped-down, almost punishing minimal cousin (bars of color, blunt monospace prose), sharing D's obsession with judgment-consistency rather than record-accuracy.

A genuine cross-link: E's "the lock" (a self-regenerating text collapsing to a fixed point) is the same finding as B's regeneration/relay work — a closed process with nothing outside it to correct against always stalls. That piece sits oddly next to E's museum pieces and reads like a graft from the other family.

**Least like the others:** F. It is the only folder with no made object of any kind — no image, chart, score, or found photograph — nothing but text. Everything else here, including the other prose-heavy folders, keeps at least an occasional picture in the room.

## 5. Most and least

**Most want to see more of: B.** It is the only practice actually building and testing a formal system across genuinely different materials (a walk, a poem, a crowd, a graph, a cast face, a river) with real, falsifiable predictions and real surprises — the rest are variations on close reading a single found document.

**Least want to see more of: C.** It retreads A's and F's territory (gaps in a record, honest refusal to invent) without either A's range and visual confidence or F's discipline and brevity — by its own admission still "not a portfolio," and it reads that way next to the others.

Six folders, two schools, and one that doesn't belong to either.
