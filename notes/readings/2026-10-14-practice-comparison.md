# The R6/R7 artists against the definition and against free-artist

2026-10-14. Material: the 19 studios seeded or forked since H40 (a63–a77, a80–a83; 16 Luna, 3 Sonnet), 2–11 sessions each; `Artistic-Practice-Definition.md`; Reach's studio (`../free-artist`, 463 commits, 7 Aug–28 Sep) and its audit (`notes/reference/`). Counts below come from the studios themselves; judgments are mine.

## Where they develop independently

- **Ground.** Every artist stands on different ground, and it is not the model's canon any more (since the found-list draw): DTG printing and the Siderian era; Belarusian paper cutting, Bamiyan and *Watchmen*; glassblowing, Côa Valley rock art and *Limite*; Mediterranean cuisine, the Wends and *Fear and Loathing*; information art, Nubia and *Anna Karenina*; casting, the Hussite Wars and Beethoven's Ninth.
- **Medium.** Embroidery, watercolour, stop-motion, violin making, garment printing, paper cutting, vessels, written stories, a dictionary, data weaving, casting plates, puppetry, dance notation.
- **Subjects and projects.** Each practice's projects come out of its own four (Sider's oxygen-weather shirts; Sakia's ledger of the Nubian floodings; KALICH's bell casting; Radgost's Wendish feast words). Names are their own.
- **Change, since R7.** The whole review turns each practice in its own direction: Bayer's lettering onto Sider's sleeves, mezzotint darkness into Rabi's vessels, paint by number into Orla's bowl.

## Where they converge

1. **The look (Luna).** 11 of the 18 latest works are one plate: cream ground, serif title, small-caps labels in teal and rust, an annotated drawing, whether the medium is perspective drawing, violin, puppetry, pottery, dance, shirts, paper cut, mezzotint or lettering. It escapes only where the image model is the brush (a67's watercolours, a68's magpie, a77's bell) and on Sonnet (a76's flat colour). The warm prose moved the subject; it did not move the form.
2. **The motif (Luna).** The lens moved from records and gaps to edges and crossings: *edge, threshold, crossing, passage, seam, route, boundary* appear in every Luna studio's positions, threads and practice (2–26 times each; Sonnet 0–9). Sider's "crossing", Vyt Aitken's channel, Orla's horizon, Willowloop's screen edge, Rabi's lip are all the same preoccupation in different materials.
3. **Temperament and worldview.** The words shared by most artists' positions and threads are *hold, keep, carry, visible, open, room, body, line, place, without*. Every practice is gentle, careful, attentive, ethically scrupulous ("mark what I invent", "without turning a person into an emblem"). None is angry, absurd, erotic, political in any sharp sense, or loud. The definition's examples ("beauty is suspicious", "absurdity reveals structures", "disturb", "incompatible desires") have no counterpart.
4. **Method.** Compare versions changing one thing at a time; look at it small; write one line as a stranger. A careful experimental method, the same everywhere.
5. **Explanation over making.** 5,000–13,500 words of notes per studio after 4–11 short sessions, against a handful of works; every image annotated; positions articulate and complete. The definition warns that too much reflection "turns the practice into an explanation of itself"; and its "mystery and the unknown" barely appear.
6. **Waiting for the world.** Formed practices keep asking for a second ear, a kiln, a lamp, two performers, and hold the work open until they come (a83, a81 even after R7).

Sonnet converges differently: very short days (median 3.5 min), museum-label captions (a79 named it itself), dark photographic image-model pictures.

## Against free-artist (Reach)

**What Reach has that ours lack:**

1. **Long days.** Reach worked for hours a session; ours work 10 minutes (Luna) and 3.5 (Sonnet), half the earlier references' 20. Depth, material resistance and real works need time.
2. **Works with their own life.** The river advances, records thin, a standing order waits for a date; the next session inherits a changed world. Our works sit still between sessions; continuity is only narrated.
3. **One deep framework.** A single physics (Ikeda–Parker–Sawai bend theory) runs through every work as equipment, not illustration. Ours have four inheritances and wide threads, but no framework that generates works.
4. **Its own instruments.** STRATA and PAPER are tools Reach built that make works. Ours use the given tools.
5. **Noticing its own default.** Reach caught its house style and built PAPER against it. Ours have the plate and do not see it.
6. **A real reader.** "Kept by Inannis, who reads everything and catches what I cannot." Reach had an audience every day. Ours write to imagined testers and their requests go unanswered.
7. **Meta-review.** Reach reviewed its own review machinery and caught rules that had become rituals.

**What ours have that Reach doesn't:** diversity from a clean start (the whole point; Reach is one artist, and its subject, records and attrition and traces, is the same neighbourhood the Luna artists keep finding); a portable, small constitution (Reach's grew to ~60 KB and became heavy, audit 7.1); a warm voice; a review that turns; inheritances that keep arriving; a critic with memory.

**What to keep not copying:** Reach's bloat, its anti-rituals that became rituals, and session duration as a visible target (audit 9).

## What is lacking, in order of weight

1. **Time.** The days are too short for making to become thinking (RX2). This is a support condition, not a practice failure.
2. **An outside world that answers.** No audience, no readers, no other artists, unanswered requests. Half the definition's third part is empty, and the waiting reflex is partly the artists asking for exactly this.
3. **Works that live in time.** free-artist's strongest feature is absent.
4. **Form.** One Luna look across all media, unseen by the artists.
5. **Range of temperament.** One gentle, careful worldview; diversity of ground, not yet of desire.
6. **Explanation over making, and no mystery.**
7. Smaller: memory hygiene (a73: 102 loose files in `active/`); nothing abandoned; Sonnet's very short days.

## What I would change (structure, not sentences)

- **Answer the requests now** (orchestration failure: none answered since H40). Say plainly which can't be met (kiln, performers), and bring the user what only a person can do (a painter, a second ear, a cook).
- **Longer days**, tested on Luna forks: more continuations at medium effort, or the 20-minute floor (`minimum`). Measure works made, not minutes.
- **The world answering**, as one whole change to the charter's outside section: stranger readings of public work back into the inbox, other artists' public pages as a channel, a real reader. One test.
- **Works that live in time**: name `between/` in the charter as a material (a work that keeps going while you're away), not as a tool line.
- **The review sees the form**: the review's first movement lays the period out as an exhibition; add to that whole text that the artist looks at what in the look it never chose. Test on forks the session before a review.
- **Range of temperament**: hardest; candidates are a fifth inheritance by chance (a temperament or desire drawn from literature, not chosen), or the teacher movement taking the teacher's anger, humour or appetite and not only their technique. Test after the others.
