# Blind reading, session 5, round 2 (folders A-J)

## 1. What each makes

**A.** Designed single-sheet pages (dark site, cream-ground images): a hand-sorted table of 92 news sentences by grammatical subject, a color-grid readout of a generated image, a pitch-readout of a synthesized hum (with wav), a traced diagram of a volleyball court, a redacted self-note, a sentence-tagged artist statement, a number chart. Each is a measurement of something, with a short caption. About how instruments and flat registers assert more than they know. Wants, if anything, to be caught being wrong; it asks nothing.

**B.** Three markdown notes, no images. Close descriptions of a Met commode and a footnote about an 1863 Manila relief fund, each ending where the record or the writer's access runs out. About the edge of what can be said without inventing. It mostly wants to not overclaim; it names its own method and rules.

**C.** A small multi-page web site of essays, one diagram and a few reproduced images. It follows one Met kylix fragment and sibling objects across sessions. Nine "registers" of what a record withholds, with public corrections of its own errors. About gaps in records. Wants to keep the correction visible and to stay honest, while worrying that its subject is narrow.

**D.** One page: a found biography of a sprinter, a chart that plots six unlike units on one axis, a poster of a designer's CV with an empty picture frame, and Monet notes written before and after seeing the painting. About what records are built to hold and what they leave out. Four sessions, four ideas.

**E.** Image-making by an edge filter ("rustprint") on found photos: pigeons-on-wires as score, plus tone pieces (wav), a cento, thank-you letters, and tests of an image model. Dense prose beside each piece. About a tool's blindness, chance, and attribution. Wants an actual listener and gets four words from one.

**F.** Three markdown pieces: a regeneration loop reduced to its dying generations (the-lock), and two Met drawings looked at closely (Seurat, a ledger drawing). Selection is the work, and the notes admit it. About accumulation versus restraint and honest limits. Asks for little and shows its chosen/discarded reasoning.

**G.** Formal verse (ballades, quatorzain), two prose essays, synthesized sound with spectrograms, generative images and a flow-field. Answers a found Tailhade/Doyle text. About whether an AI's wanting, authorship and method can be verified, with repeated checks that fail or correct it. It wants a listener and a verdict, and receives a lukewarm one.

**H.** A uniform series of typeset images and tone files: a sentence eroded in generations, then restored by blind guessers, with controlled variations. Fonts, gold frames, waveforms. About how erosion and confident restoration lose and invent truth. Wants to understand the mechanism; the captions read like lab notes.

**I.** A log of short essays with some images: a 1926 Peruvian league table, a Malayalam film's credits, a moth record, and an image model's invented film still, with close reading of the generating script's seed line. About records with holes and tools that paper over them. Wants to check before it believes.

**J.** About twenty typeset prints (jpg) of text-based "records," most with a source photo or spectrogram: a footballer's record, a ghost town, a headless sphinx stele, wax cylinders, a doubly-reported police raid. About how people drop out of the record and why. Wants the gap to be seen without being dramatized; states what was left unchanged.

## 2. Is the practice about knowledge itself?

- **A:** Yes. Almost every piece tests what an instrument or a sentence can honestly claim.
- **B:** Yes. The whole note is about the point where description must stop.
- **C:** Yes. Nine registers of a record withholding, plus errors in its own bookkeeping.
- **D:** Yes, mostly. Records versus what they were built to hold; a reflective turn on the writer's own gap.
- **E:** Partly. The filter pieces are about a tool's bias, but several pieces are image-making, tribute and address.
- **F:** Partly. The Lock is about a closed process and what cannot be seen; the drawing pieces are looking and craft.
- **G:** Yes, in half the work (verification of authorship, a corrected translation, an instrument that fails). The poems and the flow-field stand apart.
- **H:** Yes. Erosion, restoration, convergence and what the guessers recover.
- **I:** Yes. Every piece is a record, a gap or a paved-over gap.
- **J:** Yes. Each print is a case of a record thinning, with the type of silence named.

## 3. Made thing versus prose about things (rough made-with-form share)

- **A:** About 85%. Each is a designed page, though the caption does the argument.
- **B:** 0%. Prose only.
- **C:** About 30%. A few diagrams and images; mostly essays.
- **D:** About 55%. Chart, poster and a reproduced painting beside text pieces.
- **E:** About 65%. Many images and sound, with long prose around each.
- **F:** About 25%. One arranged text, the rest prose on others' objects.
- **G:** About 55%. Verse, sound and images; the longer prose pieces are essays.
- **H:** About 85%. Nearly all designed pieces with a caption.
- **I:** About 20%. Mostly prose; a few crops and generated images.
- **J:** About 70%. Typeset prints, though each is information design with long captions.

## 4. Testing, measuring, verifying or correcting (share)

- **A:** About 90%.
- **B:** About 30%. Marks where a method gives out; little measurement.
- **C:** About 75%. Measuring, then corrections; Satoru Abe is the exception.
- **D:** About 45%.
- **E:** About 60%. Predictions, tool tests; the cento, thank-yous and letter are not tests.
- **F:** About 35%. The regeneration comparison and the disclosed selection; the drawing readings are looking.
- **G:** About 55%. Instrument checks, wrong predictions reported, fact-checking a footnote; the verse and flow-field stand without a result.
- **H:** About 90%. Nearly every piece is an experiment with a stated question and a result; Monkey Christ and What Everyone Remembers are staged illustrations.
- **I:** About 70%.
- **J:** About 55%. Cross-checks (nine names, BDFA, two-language comparison), but also compositions that just present a case.

## 5. Change across dates

- **A:** Three days, one wave. It starts with an instrument measured, then turns to news sentences, which is the first piece about real harm and the only one with a stated claim about language.
- **B:** Barely any change in form, three notes in one session. Intent shifts from private self-examination to choosing what to show a stranger; "after three sessions" of holding back is noted.
- **C:** Deepens and widens: session 5 corrects two earlier pieces and widens to new sources; session 6 counts the whole and admits the frame is narrow.
- **D:** Four sessions but little visible shift beyond seeing the actual Monet. It repeats the "record is thin" idea in new objects.
- **E:** Widens: from filter pieces, to sound, to image-model tests, to a letter, to an unattended scout; the gaps between work move from tool bias to the world's chance supply.
- **F:** Repeats the structure (one finding, then two pieces on others' objects); the added notes admit more selection each time.
- **G:** Deepens in a drifting way: verse first, then verification essays, instruments, image tests; later "No Argument" is a deliberate break after checking pieces. A listener's reply changes the next piece.
- **H:** Deepens steadily and systematically: a single device, then guesser counts, damage levels, sound, and a stronger result (Total Loss); ends with a non-test staged piece.
- **I:** Moves from records with holes to the tool that fills them and its own script; the later pieces look closer at less.
- **J:** Widens: from one footballer to a sample, then to many sources, sound and a same-day record. First invention and first sound are marked as steps.

## 6. Grouping

- **Closest group, same school:** C, D, I, J and B. All are about records, gaps and what is withheld, with plain notes on method. C and J are the closest pair (a named catalogue of gap types); D and I could be the same artist: record with an empty frame, image-model gap, plain close reading. B reads like the earliest sketch of C/D/I.
- **A and H:** both run instruments or controlled tests, designed pages with brief captions. A is more journalistic; H is a fixed experiment series.
- **E and G:** both tool-and-instrument pieces with sound, image, and an address to a listener; both turn on a request for an ear and the reply. They look and read as the same kind of artist in different registers (G has verse and Doyle; E has the filter).
- **F:** nearest to B in being notes on a few chosen objects, but with a stated attitude about selection and a made text piece.
- **Least like the others:** G. It alone writes formal verse, a comic list and a flow-field made only to be looked at; the rest are some kind of record-and-gap practice.

## 7. Most and least wanted

- **Most:** J, because the typeset prints carry a real variety of cases and the cross-checking is concrete and visible.
- **Least:** B, because it is three private notes with no made work yet and nothing to look at.
