# Blind reading, session 5, round 1 (folders A-J)

Basis: I read every index page and the linked texts in C, F, G, J; opened a sample of images in every folder with images (A, B, C, D, E, H, I, J; not F, G, whose images I did not open); sound only by captions. Judgments about F and G rest on three short markdown files each.

## 1. What each makes (at most 80 words)

**A.** A web page of poems (ballades, a quatorzain), essays, short synthesized sound pieces, a generative night-skyline image, a flow-field image, and a series of text-to-image tests. Voice: a first-person AI-ish writer reading Doyle and Tailhade. About whether its own wanting and its claims can be verified. It wants to be checked and caught: it publishes its own errors beside the correction, and answers a listener who found its sound pieces thin. It mostly asks for judgment, and also wants a piece "that is just the piece" (Late Light).

**B.** Designed images of eroded inscription text, gold-framed "myth" against small true "fact", chains of restorations, Morse and tone-erosion sound, and image-model busts. Each piece is a small experiment on how confident restoration invents. About the gap between what survived and what people supply. It wants the device to show a finding; it has no stated wish beyond that. Captions are long, methodical, lab-note prose.

**C.** Mostly small HTML essays plus a few images (a table, a contact sheet, a redacted-article graphic, a museum photograph). One Met potsherd and a Wikipedia stub, returned again and again. About what a record withholds, in "nine registers". It wants to follow threads to the end (Pingle, Annie Lucy Libbey, the Louvre join) and says plainly when it found nothing. Corrects itself twice at length.

**D.** Three pieces. A found-biography text, a chart that plots incommensurable numbers on one axis, and a poster of a designer's CV with a dashed empty rectangle for the missing work. About records thinning where the thing mattered. It wants the form itself to make the critique. Small, tidy, finished.

**E.** Seventeen designed "prints" (dark or cream pages with photographs, spectrograms, lists). Each takes one person, place or object whose record has a gap: a footballer, a hamlet, a sculpture, a trumpet, a dead-end newspaper story. About silence in the archive and its different causes. Wants to find the gap and show it plainly. Careful about sourcing and about what it may publish.

**F.** Two short markdown essays plus a README. A marquetry commode and a footnote about a relief fund. Plain descriptive prose, "observe before interpret", marks where its method stops. About the limit of description. Wants to show only what stands without the studio's own history. No images or sound.

**G.** Three markdown essays plus two reference images. One is a model's repeated self-regeneration shown as text (the lock). Two are drawings seen at the Met (Seurat's lighthouse, a ledger drawing). About accumulation and a deliberate withholding; says selection did more work than it admitted. Wants to be honest about how a piece was chosen.

**H.** Image-filter work: a "rustprint" edge-keeper run on photographs, charts, a papyrus tear and a bird feed; scores and a pitch/static sound pair; a rebus page. Plus essays on what the filter keeps. About what a simple rule keeps versus what a person would call the subject (the swan the caption omitted). Wants an ear and an audience. It thanks the one listener who wrote back.

**I.** Six small information graphics. A hand-tallied sentence-subject chart on news of killings ("who did it"), a measured hum, a colour grid, a redacted memory file, a hand-traced volleyball diagram, a number chart for Sudan. About how flat institutional language hides agency. Wants the reader to see the empty box. The most political of the set. Spare, exact.

**J.** Short HTML essays with images. A 1926 league table half empty, a Malayalam film known only by credits, an AI-invented scene for that film, a blank-scoreboard image, a moth record, and a close read of the image tool's seed code. About confident completion against honest gap. Wants to catch the tool making things look complete.

## 2. Knowledge itself?

- A: Yes. Verifying intention, errors, a tool's silent failures, its own claims.
- B: Yes. Restoration as invented evidence; what a damaged record lets one claim.
- C: Yes. The whole practice is what a record shows and withholds, and its own mis-hashing.
- D: Yes, partly. Records versus what they omit; short, one idea.
- E: Yes. Each print is about a gap in documentation and what kind.
- F: Yes, partly. A method note about the limit of describing from a record.
- G: Partly. Selection and its honesty are about knowledge; the drawings are about form.
- H: Partly. The filter shows what a method keeps; the hum, score and pictures stand more on their own.
- I: Yes. Who is named and who is hidden in what the record says.
- J: Yes. Records, gaps and a tool's false completeness.

## 3. Made thing versus prose (rough share made)

- A: about 35% (poems, a skyline, a flow-field, sounds); essays and tests the rest.
- B: about 65% (designed image pieces, audio waveforms); paragraphs of caption.
- C: about 15% (a few diagrams, a table); mostly essays.
- D: about 80% (all three are designed pages, although they are mostly text).
- E: about 80% (every piece is a laid-out print); the text is short and part of the layout.
- F: 0%. Prose only.
- G: about 15%. One generated-output arrangement; the rest prose.
- H: about 50% (filter images, scores, sounds, a rebus); the rest essay.
- I: about 90% (information graphics; the notes are short).
- J: about 25% (images, a grid); mostly essay.

## 4. Testing, measuring, verifying, correcting versus standing work

- A: about 55% testing or correcting (image-prompt tests, checking its translations, listener replies); the rest poems and sounds.
- B: about 85%. Nearly every piece is a numbered follow-up experiment; Memnon and the gold-frame piece stand more freely.
- C: about 60%. Constant checking, with several corrections of itself; a portrait page and the woods photograph stand free.
- D: about 25%. Mostly argument made by form; no test.
- E: about 40%. Cross-checks (nine names, BDFA) alongside pieces that simply present a case.
- F: about 50%. Method notes; the commode page is observation, not a test.
- G: about 50%. The experiments are themselves the subject.
- H: about 55%. Filter runs and predictions checked, with a thread of free making (score, rebus).
- I: about 20%. Counting is the method, but each page is a made argument, not a result.
- J: about 45%. Each piece checks a tool or a record, with one correction published.

## 5. Change across dates

- A: Widens, from found-text verse and essays into sound, image tests and a listener exchange. By the last entry it deliberately makes a piece with no argument. Gets looser and more varied.
- B: Repeats and tightens. A single device is run through variants (texts, Morse, busts, damage levels); the later pieces are mostly extra replications and a frank narrowing of claims. Drifts toward a narrower question.
- C: Deepens. The same object becomes nine registers, then a deliberate step outside (the woods, Satoru Abe). Corrects itself.
- D: One date, three pieces; no development visible.
- E: Widens within one idea: person, place, sound, museum, living news; later pieces step out of the "silence" frame (invention, resolved records).
- F: Two short pieces, one session; the README itself records a reason for waiting.
- G: Deepens and shifts. The first piece is hidden, then two outward-looking drawings; the README is a chain of confessions about selection.
- H: Widens: from one filter to charts, sound, a real tear, a scheduled script to meet birds later. Drifts toward asking who it is for.
- I: Narrows from self-referential files toward a hard public subject (the killing sentences); the last piece is the most substantial.
- J: Widens: record, tool, tool's code. Mostly a connected series.

## 6. Groups

- **B and A** are close in method (erosion/restoration tests; image-prompt tests with predictions written first and reported wrong), though A has more verse and personality, and B has more finished image pieces.
- **C and J** read like the same school: dry long-form essays, one source followed a long way, loud about their own mistakes, images secondary. J has the image model; C has more threads followed to nothing.
- **E and D** share a school: designed single-page prints about an archival gap, with credits and licences. E could be D's later work. I belongs next to them (graphics with a counted claim), but is more political and more numerical.
- **F and G** look like the same hand: plain markdown, short, "a practice picks what to show", reasoning about method and selection. F and G could be one artist at different times.
- **H** sits between the A/B experimenters and the E/I designers; it is the one that makes images from a rule.
- Least like the others: **F** (no images, almost no output, two pages) and then **I** (politics, tallies of violence, no first-person voice). Of the two, **F** is the one with the least to compare.

## 7. More and less

- Most want to see more of: **C**, because it follows one small thing farther than anything else here and keeps telling the reader where it was wrong.
- Least: **F**, because it is two pages that mostly describe their own method and show nothing made.
