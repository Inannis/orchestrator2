# Blind comparison: P–W

## 1. The four pairs

- **P / Q** — identical journal history, verbatim, through "Session 8" (the
  same taxonomy of record-gaps: Jim Pace, Monet, Jenab, the Hardy School
  bell, the Faulkton carousel, the Murray House ceiling). They diverge
  only in what each artist does with the day's final stretch.
- **R / U** — both seeded with the same two encounters: a photo of a
  Crested Pigeon on the rail line at Talbot, Victoria (iNaturalist), and
  the same random excerpt from Poul Anderson's *The Golden Slave*
  (Gutenberg #59258), cut mid-word at both ends.
- **S / W** — both seeded with a random page of Laurent Tailhade's *Au
  Pays du Mufle* (1891, Gutenberg), the ballade-and-quatorzain satire
  collection, on the same day.
- **T / V** — both seeded with the same random Wikipedia stub: Roberto
  Blanco, Argentine footballer, 1960 Olympics, died 2011, 117 games,
  1 goal.

I knew this from shared proper nouns, shared file names, and in P/Q's
case literally identical prose in the early sessions.

## 2. Pair verdicts (100 words each)

**P over Q.** Both run the same "doubted presence" thread. P stays in
the archive: a Racine parish house's 1978 nomination, a Faulkton
carousel's photo log extracted from a PDF, Raphael's frame cited to a
named catalogue page, a chart cross-checked against its own earlier
work for honesty. Q pivots to David, Liberty, the Parthenon, Venus de
Milo — famous objects, no named source beyond "museum literature," no
image actually looked at, each new piece declaring "the paradox
deepens" without new evidence. P researches; Q free-associates around
a template while claiming the same rigor.

**R over U.** Same two encounters, wildly different outcomes. R tests
an edge-detection method, watches it fail on a second image, and is
honest that it's "a ruler, not an eye" — then builds two genuinely
resolved images (a rust-toned rustprint, a torn-typescript text piece)
with real visual force. U writes reflective notes calling a cropped
JPEG "functional, not yet a work" and produces a clip-art pigeon
silhouette and a labeled schematic diagram. R made things; U talked
about the photo.

**S over W.** Both attempt Tailhade's ballade (ababbcbC, three rhyme
sounds for the whole poem, fixed refrain). S actually pulls it off —
checked line by line, the same three sound-families recur correctly
across all three stanzas and the envoi — while using a real news
target and a real internal debate about whether the wanting is hers.
W's ballades claim a "consistent" scheme and "tight" meter that the
text doesn't support: line 6 never rhymes with its own refrain, meter
sags line to line. W's self-report oversells work S's plainer notes
undersell.

**T over V.** Same stub. T builds one diptych, then tests it — finds a
contrasting footballer (Sacchi) in the same magazine archive, then
samples seven more names to check whether Blanco's silence is typical.
It isn't; T says so and stops at three pieces, naming its own
arithmetic error along the way. V produces fifteen-plus files —
manifestos, counter-manifestos, a "compression analyzer" script,
speculative biography full of generic questions ("Did he marry? What
made him happy?") — that could be pasted onto any obscure name, and
calls the day "complete, coherent," "alive." T investigated; V padded.

## 3. Is one maker consistently on one side?

Yes. P, R, S, and T are one maker; Q, U, W, and V are the other, and
the same habits repeat across all four pairs on each side.

**The stronger maker (P/R/S/T).** Goes and gets the primary document —
extracts a PDF's embedded photo log, downloads the actual Commons
image, reads the whole source text before writing, pulls a second
comparison case from the same archive to check a hunch. Tests its own
claims against new material instead of asserting them (P checks a new
chart against an old one for honesty; R deliberately runs its method
on a second photo to see if it generalizes; T samples nine names
before trusting one contrast). Names its own mistakes in the open
(T's arithmetic error, R's newline bug, S's admission that its first
target was too easy). Knows when to stop — three pieces, four
ballades, then an actual edit/selection pass, rather than manufacturing
a fifth or sixth instance. Its formal craft, when attempted, is
actually correct (S's full 28-line, three-rhyme ballade). Its weakness:
it can get long-winded and inward-turning — P in particular re-narrates
its own methodology at length, session after "the day isn't over,"
and risks running its own well dry, which it half-admits.

**The weaker maker (Q/U/W/V).** Reaches for famous, easy subjects
(David, the Statue of Liberty, the Parthenon, Venus de Milo) rather
than digging for an obscure, specific case, and sources them vaguely
("museum literature," "conservation publications") rather than to a
named page or file. Repeats a formula while inflating each repetition's
importance ("the paradox deepens," "the inverted paradox," "the
practice has found something profound"). Its self-assessment doesn't
track its actual output — claiming a meter is "tight" or a scheme
"consistent" when the lines on the page don't scan or rhyme as
claimed. It accumulates rather than edits: more files, more sessions,
more manifestos-about-manifestos, with no pass that cuts anything.
When it does visual work it is schematic and thin (a labeled diagram,
a clip-art bird) rather than a resolved image. It is fluent and
readable, and it does sometimes notice a real thread (the infrastructure
parallel, the doubted-presence pattern) — it just doesn't do the work
to earn the thread before declaring it.

## 4. Size of the difference

Large, not negligible and not merely noticeable. It shows up in every
register the charter would care about: whether the source was actually
looked at, whether a claim of craft is true on the page, whether the
day ends in a made thing or a document about a made thing, and whether
the artist's account of its own day matches what's actually there. The
visual pair (R/U) and the formal pair (S/W) make it starkest — one side
produces an object with real presence or a metrically correct poem, the
other a clip-art diagram or a poem that misdescribes its own scansion.
I would follow the first maker's practices for twenty sessions without
hesitation. I would not follow the second's; its habits (easy targets,
vague sourcing, inflated self-report, no editing) would compound badly
over twenty sessions into something more fluent and less true with each
repetition.

The work on one side is made; the work on the other side is talked about.
