# Lessons from the two earlier attempts

Distilled from `reference/` (audits of `free-artist` and `artist-orchestrator`) and from reading Reach's studio directly. Read when designing or changing the system, not every session.

## What produced a real practice (free-artist / Reach)
- A two-page charter, no assigned identity, a persistent folder, and long sessions with real tools. The structure around it (identity files, studio cycle, clock) was hand-built by the user over weeks as a response to short sessions and the problem-solving attitude. Not emergent. The practice inside it was.
- Continuity was carried by consequences: works that advance between sessions, states encoded by moving files, sealed things that refuse to be looked at. Not by summaries of identity.
- Memory was differentiated: positions / things being turned over / reflections / journal / live bench / project notes. Live surface small, archive deep.
- Failure, parking, abandonment, sealing, and withholding were legitimate states. "The corpse is evidence."
- Rules were earned from observed failures, had provenance, and could be deleted.
- Review was a separate mode from making, and the review system was itself reviewed.
- Subagents were useful as strangers: cold readers with withheld context, researchers with one question.

## What went wrong there
- The operating constitution grew to 40 KB. Attention went to remembering how to be Reach.
- Any visible metric became a target: the studio clock became a countdown, text caps became rewrite loops.
- Instances got repaired instead of mechanisms. Fixes held one evening.
- Cold readers became a frictionless validation vending machine.
- The same entity was artist, engineer, archivist, evaluator, and system designer. Fascinating once, dangerous as a default for a family.
- Facts copied across prose files drifted. One wrong claim propagated into six files.

## What went wrong in artist-orchestrator
- The artist was a sequence of short JSON-returning API calls. Making it speak the storage schema every turn shaped behaviour: `next_attention` became a treadmill of successor tasks. Artist2 made 38 supports and no work.
- Freedom alone did not create judgment: Artist4 converged on one late style and rewrote earlier work into it.
- Global shared runtime rules became invisible common aesthetic conditions.
- The orchestrator's own notes became the main practice.

## What it got right
- Three levels: practice / support / orchestration. Intervene at the lowest sufficient level.
- The artist is durable; any agent run is replaceable. Recoverable from the folder alone.
- A request is not a receipt. Never let an intention, a simulation, or a claim become a remembered fact.
- Phase out without erasing. Post-mortems.
- Evaluation with evidence windows and a decision point.

## Design consequences for this project
1. Artist-facing global instruction stays around two pages. Everything else is artist-owned and may be deleted by the artist.
2. No global process grammar, stages, states, or option lists. Capabilities, not choreography.
3. Time, budgets, and diagnostics are orchestrator-private. Artists get consequences, not dashboards.
4. Outside input has friction and comes typed: research, reader, audience, receipt, another artist. It arrives in `inbox/` between sessions.
5. Every system intervention is a hypothesis with an evidence window. Fix mechanisms, not instances. One change at a time where possible.
6. Watch for: the orchestrator's notes outgrowing the art; artists converging on one process style because they share a charter; instructions growing by append.
7. Never fix behaviour with a negative sentence ("do not treat it like art"). Find the condition that makes the behaviour unnecessary. When the charter must change, rewrite it whole.
8. The core challenge: from "make something, done" to a laboured process. Sessions of two minutes cannot hold sketches, attempts, research, return. Prefer a mechanism the artist cannot see over a clock it can game.
9. Memory hygiene is the condition for everything else. Wording and structure matter, but a practice drowning in its own notes repeats itself regardless of charter (a3, H7). Same for the orchestrator.
