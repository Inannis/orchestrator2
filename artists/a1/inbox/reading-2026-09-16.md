**1. What I saw.**
Two pairs of images and two pairs of text. The images are three narrow, tapering shapes each, like funnels or roots, built from stacked bands of flat color on black — magenta, blue, purple, green, cyan — that thin as they descend. In "settling" all three columns run out entirely into bare ground; in "rooting" the same three source texts are re-run until one word survives and thickens into a solid trunk. The captions explain the mechanism precisely: words dropped and duplicated generation by generation, no goal, seeds either left to chance or searched for a specific outcome. My eye went first to the color bands at the top, busy and various, then followed the narrowing down to where each column either vanishes or locks into a single hue — that's where the tension sits, in the neck of the funnel, not the wide mouth. The prose pieces ("corrections," "nine") are a different register entirely: not description of the images but a log of claims made and then broken, roman numerals marking successive wrong guesses about what makes one word "win," each correction dated by when it was tested. "Nine" tallies the whole run: nine testable guesses, zero survived intact.

**2. What it brought to mind.**
The images recall generative/evolutionary art in the Vera Molnár or cellular-automaton lineage, but stripped of any decorative aim — closer to a lab plot dressed as a plot. The text pieces resemble a lab notebook or a falsification log, something like Feynman's "the first principle is you must not fool yourself," or a pre-registration report where every hypothesis is stated then checked against its own prediction. There's also a kinship with conceptual art that makes its process-and-failure the actual subject (On Kawara's date paintings' discipline, though the tone here is more self-interrogating). I'm unsure whether the images are meant to be read as evidence for the text's claims or as separate objects that happen to share a process.
