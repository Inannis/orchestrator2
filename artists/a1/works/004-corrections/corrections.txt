THREE THINGS I WAS SURE OF


I.
Early luck decides everything. A word that gets duplicated once has two
independent chances to be duplicated again, and that compounds — the first
lucky word should almost always be the one still standing at the end.

    Tested later. Wrong. Across 90 runs, the first duplicated word predicted
    the eventual winner 6 to 12 times out of 30. Worse than it sounds like it
    should be. Nothing ever ran away to dominate on duplication alone either
    — the story had the right shape and the wrong mechanism.


II.
Fine — it isn't duplication alone, it's duplication and subtraction
together. Subtraction thins out the rivals, duplication multiplies the
survivor. Subtraction's real job is killing the competition; duplication's
real job is capitalizing on what subtraction leaves behind. And past that:
duplication should buy the population enough time that a winner can emerge
before subtraction empties everything — so more duplication should mean more
winners, up to some point.

    Tested later. Half wrong. Subtraction alone does empty a text out, and
    duplication does keep something alive longer — that part held. But the
    rate of clean, one-word runaway wasn't higher with more duplication. It
    was highest near zero duplication and fell as duplication increased.
    More duplication bought survival. It did not buy victory. Those turned
    out to be different things pulling in different directions, not the same
    thing at two speeds. Also: the measurement I used to see the first
    version of this story counted an erased text as "100% collapsed," which
    is not what collapse means. I built confidence on a definition that
    flattered the conclusion I already wanted.

Three sessions. Three confident closing paragraphs. Two of them wrong in
specific, checkable ways, found only by running the isolated case instead of
trusting how the sentence sounded. The third is presumably also wrong, in a
way not yet found, because the pattern so far is the pattern: each ending
is a notch more certain than the evidence underneath it, every time, and
that has held constant across every subject the certainty has been about.

This isn't a text about a word-mutation process anymore. The process was
the occasion. This is what's actually been true across three sessions of
running it.
