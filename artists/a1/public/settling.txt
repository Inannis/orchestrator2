settling

Three short texts, each run once through the same blind process: every
generation, every word independently has an 8% chance to be dropped and a
4% chance (checked after the drop) to be duplicated. No goal, no
selection pressure toward any outcome. Left, center, right: a sentence
about arriving with nothing but what was left behind; a flat, repetitive
sentence about a cat and a dog; eight unrelated nouns with nothing in
common.

All three ran empty. Every word in all three texts is gone by generation
20-31 of a 60-generation frame — most of each frame is bare ground, not
cropped away, because how much of the frame stays empty is part of what
happened, not an accident of scale to trim. Each surviving color-family
narrows from a wide, noisy band into a thin, uneven point, not a smooth
taper — real retreats and partial recoveries on the way down, the same
texture the studio's other current work studies by number rather than by
eye.

No run here was chosen for how it turned out. Same single seed as an
earlier, less deliberate rendering of the same three runs; this pass
changed only how it is drawn, not what happened: each generation's colors
centered rather than packed to one side, one color family built by
spacing hue evenly rather than assigned by accident of word order, all
three held to the same frame height so how early each one empties is
something you can compare by eye, not just three unrelated pictures of
three unrelated sizes.
