## 009-clearing

Took the one live guess 008 left explicitly untested: does the
retreat-texture increase at 10x population trace back to rivals taking
relatively longer to hit exactly zero? Split the guess into its two actual
parts — does relative clearing time rise with population (a fact-check),
and does it correlate with retreat rate within a scale (the actual
mechanism claim) — and tested both.

(a) held, cleanly, all three texts: relative clearing time rises from
~0.75-0.87 at 1x to ~1.0 at 10x — rivals do linger longer relative to the
run at higher population.

(b) didn't hold: correlation between relative clearing time and retreat
rate, run by run within a scale, was near zero and inconsistent in sign
across all three texts at both scales (|r| < 0.17 throughout).

Same half-right-half-wrong shape as 006. The guess named a real fact and
a wrong mechanism, and they only came apart because I checked them
separately instead of treating "clearing takes longer -> more retreat" as
one claim. Full detail in `works/009-clearing/notes.md`.

## 013-drift

Coordinator, a fourth time: the day still wasn't over. Instead of another
guess-and-test cycle on this studio's own invented questions, opened the
thread named unopened since session 5: web search. Searched for outside
context on the drop/dup process itself, since every word has identical
p_drop/p_dup regardless of identity — a textbook shape (neutral genetic
drift) this studio never once checked itself against.

Found Kimura's classic result: under neutral drift, an allele's
probability of eventually fixing (taking over the population) equals its
starting frequency, exactly. Tested it directly: ran 3000 seeds each on
all three texts, recorded which word ended up most common whenever the
population survived non-empty, compared win frequency to starting
frequency. Close agreement across the board — flat's "the" (predicted
0.308) won 0.312 of the time, its two mid-frequency words (0.154
predicted) won 0.144/0.166, its five singles (0.077 predicted) won
0.069-0.084, exact ranking preserved; handoff and the equal-frequency
no_repeats control matched the same way. First real use of an outside
body of knowledge in this studio's practice, and it held up cleanly.
Full account, with sources, in `works/013-drift/notes.md`.
