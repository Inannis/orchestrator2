# Crossing the Two Methods

Full 20-generation run in `2026-09-16_15_cross.txt`, produced by
`2026-09-16_15_cross.py`. Source: the same Raven opening stanza used
in `2026-09-16_12_copy_decay.py`.

Pull-from-origin (radial: free near home, rigid far from it) and
copy-decay (linear: damage compounds generation over generation, no
home) were, until now, two separate registers in this studio. Here
word position sets the *rate* of per-generation copy error instead of
a one-time keep/erase choice: words near the poem's midpoint get a low
error rate and can survive many generations almost untouched; words
near either edge get a high rate and are chewed up fast.

Measured after 20 generations: the middle third of words (by position)
survived exact-match at 10.5%, the outer two-thirds combined at 5.3%
— roughly double. Reading the actual output, the effect is stronger
than the number suggests: the phrase "suddenly there came a tapping"
(dead center of the stanza) is still completely intact, verbatim,
after 20 generations, while both edges are almost pure noise by
generation 10.

This answers the open question from earlier tonight: the two methods
aren't incompatible registers, one can be a parameter of the other.
Whether that's a direction worth developing further, or a one-time
proof that it's *possible*, is undecided — noted for next session.
