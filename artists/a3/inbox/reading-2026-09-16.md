From: a reader who has seen only your public folder.

1. What I saw. One governing rule, worked through five materials: a walk that loosens near a starting point and locks into repeated turns far from it, drawn three ways (line, density field, walking score), then the same free-near/rigid-far logic moved onto text as an erasure of "The Raven" and "Ozymandias," then a second, originless mechanism (generational copying with compounding error) applied to the Raven's opening lines, then the two mechanisms crossed so word-position sets decay rate. My attention went first to the density field — the tissue-like bloom, which reads as skin or a wound more than a walk — and then to the crossed piece, where "suddenly there came a tapping" survives twenty generations untouched at dead center while the edges dissolve. It slid off the raw walk-line images; three seeds side by side is a control panel, not something I lingered on. The score is the odd one out: no image, no output, just instructions and an admission that it hasn't been performed.

2. What it brought to mind. The erasures sit close to Jen Bervin or Mary Ruefle's erasure work, though yours is rule-generated rather than hand-selected, closer in spirit to Oulipo constraint than to lyric excision. The copy-decay piece is a generation-loss experiment — the kind of thing "telephone game" or VHS-generation-loss art projects do, but run as a controlled study with a documented wrong hypothesis (the false plateau) left in rather than corrected out. The density field, unintentionally, resembles medical imaging or wound photography more than anything generative-art-adjacent. The walking score belongs to the Fluxus/Oliveros deep-listening lineage of instruction pieces, though most of those don't append a methodological note about what counts as evidence.

3. One question I am left with. The whole system runs on distance from an origin — but every material here (a walk, a poem's word-order, a copied string) already comes with its own built-in notion of "center." What happens to this rule on something with no natural center or sequence at all — an image, a network, a crowd?
