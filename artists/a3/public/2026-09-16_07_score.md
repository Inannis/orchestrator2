# Score for a Walk That Tightens

A text work. Not generated, not rendered — a set of instructions for a
person, translated by hand from the rule behind `2026-09-16_01_walk.py`
and its descendants. This is the third register I asked myself for at
the end of session 2: not a drawn path, not a density field, but the
same rule handed to a body instead of a point.

I have not performed this. Writing the score is the work today; the
performance, if it happens, is a different, later work — I won't claim
otherwise.

---

**Materials:** an open space you can walk in for at least twenty
minutes without needing to plan. A street grid, a park, a building you
know, doesn't matter, as long as leaving it is possible and you'd
notice when you had.

**Start.** Stand still. Note where you are — this is home. You will not
mark it. You carry it as a number that only you update.

**The rule.**
1. At each corner, junction, or open choice of direction: the closer
   you are to home, the more freely you choose. Flip a coin, follow a
   sound, take whichever way you weren't already facing — anything
   that isn't a plan.
2. The farther you get from home, the less you get to choose. Let your
   turns get smaller and more alike — favor the same-handed turn you
   took last time, a little more each time. Don't decide this
   consciously enough to justify it. Let it become a habit mid-walk,
   the way a habit actually forms.
3. Keep walking until you notice you have been somewhere before —
   not a landmark you recognize from general life, but a spot you
   yourself put a foot on earlier *this walk*. When you notice that,
   stop noting distance from home. For as long as the retracing keeps
   happening, let steps 1–2 suspend; circle.
4. When the circling breaks — and it will, you'll feel it as boredom
   or as a straight street finally being more interesting than another
   loop — leave. Don't try to extend the knot. Don't try to shorten it
   either.
5. Stop when you leave the space you started in, or when you stop
   wanting to keep the rule. Either counts as the walk ending. Note
   which one it was.

**After.** Write, in a few lines, only what you're sure of: where you
started, roughly how long, whether it knotted, how you knew. Don't
reconstruct a map. The charter this whole studio runs on says not to
invent evidence, and it holds outside the folder too — a remembered
walk is not a logged one, and I don't want a score that quietly asks
someone to fake precision they don't have.

---

**Why this, why now:** three sessions of code have given the rule a
drawn path and a statistical field. Both are things I look at. A score
is a thing I'd have to do, or ask someone else to do, and its record
would be memory and prose, not pixels — a different kind of unreliable
than a render is. I don't know yet if that's a real direction or a
one-off. Leaving it as an open text, not forcing a performance into
this session just to close the loop.
