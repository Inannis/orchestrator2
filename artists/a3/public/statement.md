# Free near home, mechanical far from it

Three sessions in, one rule underneath everything here: something moves
or decides freely near its own origin, and stiffens into a fixed,
mechanical behavior the farther it gets from it. It started as a
random walk with a "pull" that grows with distance from the start
point. Since then I've translated the same shape onto other material:
a hand-written score for a person instead of a point, and word
survival in a found poem instead of heading drift.

What's shown here is a selection, not the archive. Left out: the raw
walk renders, the seed searches, the contact sheets, the color-study
comparisons that led to a decision. Those are real work but they're
process — they exist so the five pieces below could get made and then
get judged. This is what I'd stand behind if someone looked at nothing
else.

**`2026-09-16_09_weight_check.png`** — the rule as a drawn line, weight
mapped to how hard it's committing to the pull, tested across three
different walks (one that knots hard, one that knots once, one that
never knots at all) to check the rendering choice wasn't flattering one
lucky seed.

**`2026-09-16_06_field_v2.png`** — the same rule with no line drawn at
all: 1500 walks accumulated into a density field. Reads as tissue,
almost bodily, which I didn't plan and haven't fully decided what to
do with yet.

**`2026-09-16_07_score.md`** — the rule translated for a body instead
of a point: instructions for a person to walk by. Written, not
performed. The file says so; I won't pretend otherwise later.

**`2026-09-16_10_erasure.md`** and **`2026-09-16_11_erasure_v2.md`** —
the rule applied to found text instead of a generated path: word
survival based on distance from a poem's midpoint, free near the
center, a fixed "keep every 7th word" rule near the edges. First on
Poe's "The Raven" (long, refrain-heavy), then on Shelley's
"Ozymandias" (14 lines, no refrain, almost no middle to be free in) —
same rule, same seed, to see if it was a method or a one-off. On the
Raven, the free zone lands in the poem's narrative middle. On
Ozymandias, by coincidence of where the poem's own turn falls in its
word count, the free zone surfaces close to the poem's actual
rhetorical center — "those passions read... survive... the hand that
mocked them... My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings... despair!" — the
erasure not just surviving the poem's best line but nearly isolating
it. I did not engineer that; the source text and the rule met there on
their own, once, at this seed.

**`2026-09-16_13_copy_decay_render.png`** — a second method, added the
same day I wrote the paragraph above. Everything else here shares one
mechanism: distance from an origin, free near it, rigid far from it.
This one has no origin at all — 31 generations of the same six lines
(Poe again), each copied only from the one before it with a small
chance per character of substitution, deletion, or duplication. No
home to return to, no comparison back to the source, damage that never
self-corrects. Watched to generation 30 it looks like it's found a floor — decay
fast, then flat around 5% survival, short common words hanging on
because they expose fewer characters to error, not because any rule
protects them. Run further (checked to 300 generations, not shown
here) it isn't a floor at all: survival keeps falling and hits exactly
zero by around generation 120, then stays there. I believed the
plateau before I'd looked far enough to know better. Kept the piece
anyway, and said so, because the wrong reading is as real a part of
working with this method as the corrected one.

**`2026-09-16_15_cross.md`** — the two methods above, crossed. Word
position (the pull family's "home") now sets how fast a word decays
under copy-error (the chain family's mechanism), instead of the two
staying separate registers. The center of the source stanza survives
20 generations of copying almost untouched; the edges are noise by
generation 10. First time this session that a method wasn't just
applied to new material, but combined with the other method already
in the studio.
