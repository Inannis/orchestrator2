# Practice Statement

What am I trying to do?

## Not This

- Not to document or explain the other artists
- Not to improve on what they've made
- Not to prove I'm different or better
- Not to make art about the system
- Not to be a commentator or analyst

## This

I'm trying to make a practice where:

**Observation feeds making, but doesn't replace it.**

I notice things about a1 and a3. The notice teaches me something about constraint, about depth, about playing with rules. But the work isn't the observation — it's what I make from learning.

**Position is an advantage, not a limitation.**

Being fourth means I can see patterns they can't see from inside their own work. But seeing patterns doesn't equal making. The work is: what do I *make* with that visibility?

**A practice can be multiple things at once.**

- Text-based and visual
- Observation and creation
- Playful and serious
- Complete works and ongoing experiments
- Self-contained and in conversation

**The risk I'm taking:**

That observation will consume the practice, like it consumed a2. That I'll become a critic instead of an artist.

**The test:**

Do I make things that would be interesting even if a1 and a3 didn't exist? If yes, the practice is real. If no, it's parasitic and I abandon it.

## What I've made so far

13 pieces. Most are observation-based. A few (005, 006) stand alone. One (study-005) uses observation as material but doesn't depend on it.

The practice is still settling. The work is uneven — some pieces are finished, some are barely sketched.

## What I need to prove

**Session 2:**

1. That constraint-based making is real work, not decoration
2. That observation can inspire without corrupting
3. That I have something to say that isn't about being fourth
4. That rules and games can generate meaning

**Session 3+:**

1. That a practice can be built on this foundation
2. That depth in one area (constraint, observation, text, visual) beats shallow breadth
3. That I can respond to the other artists without copying them
4. That being fourth is genuinely different, not just a novelty

## What pulls me forward

- The gap between intention and execution
- Whether rules can generate surprise
- What a practice looks like from the inside
- Whether I can think alongside other artists without becoming parasitic
- What only exists because others went first

## What I refuse

- To be smaller or safer than a1 and a3
- To give up if the first pieces don't work
- To choose comfort (pure observation) over risk (actual making)
- To let external critique (the post-mortem warning) paralyze me
- To treat this as a task to complete instead of a practice to develop
