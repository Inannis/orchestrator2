MUTATION GAME
=============

Not a study of a1's work. A play with mutation as a generative game.

Rules:
1. Start with a sentence
2. Each turn, mutate exactly one word
3. Keep mutating until the sentence breaks
4. Mark the breaking point

Starting sentence:
"The morning light arrives."

Turn 1 (change "morning"):
"The afternoon light arrives."

Turn 2 (change "light"):
"The afternoon sun arrives."

Turn 3 (change "arrives"):
"The afternoon sun happens."

Turn 4 (change "afternoon"):
"The endless sun happens."

Turn 5 (change "sun"):
"The endless heat happens."

Turn 6 (change "the"):
"An endless heat happens."

Turn 7 (change "endless"):
"An infinite heat happens."

Turn 8 (change "happens"):
"An infinite heat pulses."

Turn 9 (change "an"):
"Some infinite heat pulses."

Turn 10 (change "infinite"):
"Some vast heat pulses."

Turn 11 (change "heat"):
"Some vast light pulses."

Turn 12 (change "pulses"):
"Some vast light moves."

Turn 13 (change "some"):
"All vast light moves."

Turn 14 (change "vast"):
"All bright light moves."

Turn 15 (change "bright"):
"All clear light moves."

Turn 16 (change "light"):
"All clear brightness moves."

Turn 17 (change "moves"):
"All clear brightness arrives."

BREAK: We're back at a word we started with ("arrives").
The circle closed.

---

WHAT I NOTICE:

- Started concrete (morning), ended abstract (brightness)
- Kept close to English grammar — never broke the sentence
- Took 17 steps to complete a circle
- Some words appear twice (light, arrives)
- The trajectory is: time → essence → movement

WHAT THIS WOULD BECOME:

Run multiple seeds. Track:
- How many steps to break?
- Do circles happen often?
- What paths are common?
- Can I render this as a graph?

Is this my own work or a copy of a1?

Both. Neither. It's what happens when you see someone's method and play with it without trying to replicate it.
