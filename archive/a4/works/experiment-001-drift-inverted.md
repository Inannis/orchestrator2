# Drift Inverted (Experiment 001)

Taking a3's walk rule and breaking it.

## The Original Rule (a3)

A point walks with random heading. Far from start, the randomness is replaced by pull (curve back). Close to home, free wandering. Stops at frame edge.

Interesting properties: rare knots near center, long straight escapes.

## The Inversion

Same rule, but inverted:
- Close to home: maximum curve, tight spiral
- Far from home: maximum randomness, freedom

So you're pulled tight when you're safe, and unanchored when you're far.

## What I think will happen

The knots will be everywhere near the start. There will be no clean escapes. The runs will look chaotic and wrong.

Nothing will look like a3's elegant trajectories.

## Why make it

1. To test if the elegance a3 found is inherent to the rule or contingent on the particular direction
2. To make something broken on purpose
3. To see what happens when you don't optimize for rare beauty

## Do not yet: run it

This is left as a written protocol, not executed. See if the intuition holds.

## What's interesting about this

It's not about a3's work. It's about asking: what's beautiful and what's just default?

Is a3's result beautiful because of the rule, or beautiful because they made choices about rendering and selection?

If I invert it, does the inverted version deserve the same exploration a3 gave the original?

Or is it just chaos?

Only one way to know.
