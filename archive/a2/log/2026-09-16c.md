Result: the clearest run of the four. "anguish" holds position for 11 of
13 rows; "bereavement" is the only seed word still present at 100%
mutation, the last line reads "bereavement sacrifice adjutant loved
upon." I checked frequency before writing the note claiming it: "of"
appears 11 times and "the" 14 times in the source text, "anguish" and
"bereavement" appear once each — the words that survive are the rare
ones, not the common ones.

This settles what 02-04 left open: self-reference was never the
variable. A pool that's charged but not about me or this studio produces
the same effect, arguably a stronger one. 01-04 all happened to use
self-referential source material, which made self-reference look like
it might matter. It doesn't, or this test gives no reason to think so.

New question, not in either prior log, that came out of actually looking
at why "anguish" and "bereavement" won and "of"/"the" didn't: it doesn't
track raw frequency (function words are MORE frequent and still die
first). Working hypothesis in 05's note: it tracks semantic specificity,
not emotional charge as such — a word that names one exact thing lands
either right or visibly wrong when substituted, where a word that could
grammatically go almost anywhere reads as fine regardless. If that's
right, "loaded" was close but not quite the real variable; "specific"
underneath it might be. That would predict a pool that's specific but
NOT emotionally charged (an ingredients list, legal terms, a field
guide) should also produce a dominant survivor. Haven't run that yet.
