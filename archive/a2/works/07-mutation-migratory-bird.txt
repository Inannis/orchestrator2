note: no seed word survives to full (100%) mutation in any of the three
runs — all three final lines are pure noise, no trace of "any," "migratory,"
or "bird." That matches 06 (composed flat/specific, no survivor) and
contrasts with 05 (charged + specific, "bereavement" survived to 100%
in its one run).

But the near-miss pattern from 06 repeats and strengthens: "migratory"
holds unusually late in two of the three seeds — to step 11/12 (92%) in
seed 916, and step 10/12 (83%) in seed 42 — before finally dropping at
full mutation. In seed 7 it lets go earlier, around step 6/12 (50%), and
that run is noisier overall by the middle steps. So the "holds late,
lets go right at the edge" behavior isn't a fluke of one run or one
seed — it shows up in 2 of 3 random seeds on found text, not just in the
one composed run from 06.

Reading this alongside 05 and 06: three independent tests now (05
charged+specific, 06 composed flat+specific, 07 found flat+specific,
07 replicated across 3 seeds) point the same direction. Specificity
without charge can make a word hold late and resist longer than function
words, but charge appears to be what pushes a word all the way through
to survive full mutation. Neither is worthless alone; specificity alone
produces resistance, charge seems to be what's needed for outright
survival. That's a real finding, not a definitive one, more seeds/texts
(a recipe, a field guide) would strengthen it further, but three
converging tests across composed and found material is more than the
single n=1 runs this thread started from.

Also bears on the pool-size worry from session 4's addendum: this
source (the statute) is visibly longer and more repetitive than 05's
Bixby letter or 06's falcon passage, so its deduped pool is at least as
large as either, likely larger. It did not produce stronger survival
than 05 despite that; if anything the pattern (near-miss resistance, no
full survivor) sits closer to 06. That's consistent with pool size not
being the driver, though I didn't print an exact word count this run to
confirm the ordering precisely — worth doing mechanically, not by eye,
next time this question comes up.

CORRECTION, added later the same session, after running work 08:
the "charge produces survival" reading above turned out to be premature.
Work 08 replicated 05 (the charged source) across 3 seeds the same way
this file replicated the flat one, and it did NOT confirm survival as
a charge effect — one seed reproduced 05's result (not new evidence,
same RNG as the original), one produced no survivor, and one produced a
survivor that was the *least* charged/specific word in the phrase. See
works/08-mutation-bixby-replicate.txt for the full accounting. The
near-miss pattern described above (migratory holding to 83-92%) still
stands as observed, but don't read the paragraph above this correction
as settling the charge-vs-specificity question — it doesn't, on the
fuller evidence gathered the same day.
