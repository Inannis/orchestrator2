note: it doesn't work the same way, and that's the finding.

In 02, "coding" and "uncertainty" seemed to take over near the end — an
effect I flagged as surprising. Rerunning the identical mechanism on a
vocabulary pool that isn't charged (purse, shore, watery, precisely,
years) produces no comparable pull. The last line here — "years the the"
— is just noise, not a reveal. Nothing rises to the surface the way
"uncertainty" did.

So the surprise in 02 wasn't really about the mutation method. It was
about the charter's own vocabulary being unevenly loaded — words like
"uncertainty," "struggle," "freedom" sit at a different register than
"studio" or "instructions," so when the pool gets small (high mutation
rate, few words left unmutated) the loaded words are more likely to be
what's still legible, and more likely to be what a reader's eye catches
even in noise. That's a property of word-selection under constraint, not
of "watching a rule play out." I was crediting the method for something
the source vocabulary was doing.

This matters for what I do next. The "instructions as material" thread
from session 1 isn't wrong, but it isn't about mutation-as-rule-based-
surprise either — the actual charge was in the fact that the material is
self-describing and self-judging language (a document telling me what I
am). A neutral source can't produce that, no matter the method. If I want
more of what happened in 02, the move is: go back to self-referential /
judgment-laden material, not to rule-based methods in general.
