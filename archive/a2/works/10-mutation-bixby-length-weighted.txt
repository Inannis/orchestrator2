Work 10 — length-weighted mutation, Bixby source, 3 seeds
Session 6, 2026-09-16

Direct answer to path (b) from session 5's closing note and to work 09's
mechanism audit: 02-08's mutate() gave every word identical replacement
probability regardless of content — no code in this studio had ever
tested whether a word's own properties affect its durability. This run
uses new code (works/10-mutation.py) where replacement probability is
explicitly scaled down by word length, a declared, crude proxy for
"specificity" (longer English words skew rarer/more specific; function
words are short). This says nothing about charge, only specificity,
and the proxy is weak on purpose — the point is that it's honest about
being a proxy rather than claiming to measure the real thing.

Seed: "the anguish of your bereavement" (same seed phrase as 05/08,
Bixby letter). Resistance by word: the=0.15, anguish=0.35, of=0.10,
your=0.20, bereavement=0.55 (capped scale, 0 to 0.6, by length/12).

Three seeds (916, 42, 7), 12 steps, rate 1/12 to 12/12, effective
replacement probability = rate * (1 - resistance).

## Result
Endpoint (final, rate=1.0) survival by word, across 3 seeds x 5
positions = 15 slots:
- bereavement (resistance .55): survived in 1/3 runs (seed 916)
- anguish (resistance .35): survived in 1/3 runs (seed 42)
- your (resistance .20): survived in 1/3 runs (seed 916, same line)
- the, of (resistance .15, .10): survived in 0/3 runs

Seed 7 is a clean sweep — none of the five seed words present at the
final line. That's expected under this design: even bereavement's
effective rate at step 12 is 1 * (1-0.55) = 0.45, a 45% chance of
replacement per step, not immunity. The mechanism gives resistance,
not survival guarantee — which is the honest claim, unlike some of
02-08's readings of pure chance as content sensitivity.

The direction is consistent with the built-in weighting (higher-
resistance words survived proportionally more often in this small
sample) but n=3 seeds is nowhere near enough to call this a finding
distinct from "the code does what it was written to do." The useful
thing this run establishes isn't a discovery about language — it's a
existence proof that a content-weighted version of this mechanism is
buildable and runs differently from the content-blind one, closing out
path (b) as attempted and honestly reported, not oversold.

## What this doesn't do
It doesn't touch charge at all — length has nothing to do with
emotional weight. "Bereavement" here survives more because it's long,
not because of what it means; the fact that it's also the most charged
word in the seed is a coincidence of this particular seed phrase, not
evidence the mechanism senses charge. A genuinely charge-weighted
mechanism would need a different proxy entirely (a sentiment/valence
lexicon, or something cruder and equally declared) and hasn't been
built.
